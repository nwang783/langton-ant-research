# Fixed-ray extension of the axial emitter obstruction

This is an addendum to parallel_axial_hairpin_wrapper_obstruction.md, not a change to that frozen two-orientation statement.

The same proof covers ANY of the four proper rotations of the supplied axial hairpin, at finitely many fixed centers in a prolongable emitter phi(a)=a w. Pick any orientation occurring in w and rotate coordinates so its drift ray is +x. Copies with ray -x never reach a sufficiently far positive column. Copies with ray +y or -y have fixed transverse x-width: all of their reads lie within two units of their fixed center's x-coordinate, at every depth. Choose X beyond those widths as well as the finite root/identity reads. Only +x copies reach X at late depths, and each still lowers the bounded column black-height moment by4. The proof and bound then apply with m+ equal to their multiplicity.

Thus simply adding perpendicular spokes at FIXED centers does not rescue the emitter. The open escape is genuinely remote interaction, for example a recursively moving emitter that places a vertical shuttle at centers distributed along an expanding horizontal branch. Such centers are not fixed across the refinement layer and invalidate the remote-column isolation argument.

Optionally the same finite alphabet can include reflected, R/L-complemented copies. Reflection plus color complementation preserves the standard ant's local dynamics. A reflected component's complete signed field delta is minus the reflected original delta. For a fixed ray this again transfers one black from the left side of the ray to its right side (clockwise transverse direction), exactly as for the proper-rotation component. Its extra persistent black rails do not change the delta. Hence all such dihedral variants also give the same strict transverse moment change. 

No arbitrary axial controller, changing-center assembly, or different remote bulk operator is excluded.
