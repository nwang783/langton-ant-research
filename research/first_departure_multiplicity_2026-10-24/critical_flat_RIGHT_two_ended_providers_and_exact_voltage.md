## 2. Same class is NOT the same physical provider

Choose b indices with u=v_0, v_(j+ell)=v_j-d. Write the forward provider
as F=v_j, 0<j<ell, and backward provider as G=v_i, -ell<i<0.
If they have the SAME full-drift quotient class, then i=j-ell. Therefore

    G=F+d,

not F. Both physical boundary paths together have displacement -d, and
the exterior connector must compensate by +d. Dropping this voltage
would reverse which chronological profile is possible.

Let A_F=T3(F)-T1(F)>0 be the full physical L1-to-L3 span, not its phase
remainder. Because the entire root excursion has span<P, its exterior
factor has duration strictly between 0 and P. With G=F+d:

    X: T_entry(G)-T_exit(F)=P+A_F>P, impossible;
    Y: T_entry(G)-T_exit(F)=P-A_F.

Thus a same-CLASS provider can only be Y and must satisfy

    0<A_F<P.

The two boundary factors contain exactly j and -i physical steps, so
in the same-class case they total ell. Consequently the sharper exact
clock and span condition are

    T2(u)-T1(u)=P+ell-A_F,
    ell<A_F<P.

The second inequality uses the signed root span<P. It is a genuine
old-age obligation at this one flat provider, not a bound on all stock.

No primitive drift, minimal period or bounded-age premise is used. The
inequality A_F<P is a CONSEQUENCE of this particular same-class transfer,
not a general bound on cap-four cell lifetimes.
