## 3. What ALL remote-cap replacements cannot repair

The final local cap RRRRLL, entering (-1,1,W) relative to p, has the forced
six-read geometry

    (-1,1) R, (-1,2) R, (0,2) R,
    a=(0,1) R, (-1,1) L, q=(-1,0) L,

and ends at (p,E). Therefore ANY actual prefix reaching this local cap,
regardless of its length, remote cap, arm lengths, garbage, previous writer
or initial supply, leaves q white and a black.

If the next intended word begins T=LRRL, it first reads p with L, then a
with R. If p is not black it already fails. If p is black, the a read fails:
the preceding a read was the displayed local R, and the only intervening
reads are (-1,1),q,p, all off a. These are consecutive R/R reads of one actual
cell. Consequently every remote-cap replacement retaining this local return
and an immediate T (hence the same first frame F) is impossible on EVERY
background, including infinite ones. This statement has no remote-cap length
bound and no assumption that the remote cap stays far away.

It is NOT an arbitrary-near-cap theorem. Replacing the near cap, entering a
different pose, or replacing the next outbound T could evade it. Merely
repairing the distant E46 endpoint cannot. The exact new obligation is to
carry the two-bit near state, not to assume clearing q restored the original
entry interface.
