# A completed B determines the next permanent child

22 October 2026. Independently analytically checked by the certificate auditor.

Use a parent module of sign a, opposite sign b, with face w,x,y,p and its child1 at x and child2 at y. This is the same rooted module and lamp convention as `face_module_return_lemma.md`.

A completed B packet has exactly one of these two forms:

    B1 = a G_b a^3,
    B2 = a G_b a G'_b a^2.

It leaves the parent root w colored a, so the parent is immediately reentered. The return ledger forbids another completed parent return.

After B1, the interior lamps satisfy x=y=b. On reentry the first a reaches x and calls child1. That child's previous completed-return ledger already contains G. A second G is forbidden: its next call either never returns or returns B and is immediately reentered nonreturningly. Hence the permanent child is1 and the ensuing spine exponent is1.

After B2, the last boundary read of child2 has restored x to a, while y=b. On reentry the parent advances directly through x to y. Child2 also already has a G in its ledger, and so its next call either never returns or returns B and is immediately reentered nonreturningly. Hence the permanent child is2 and the ensuing spine exponent is2. In particular, there is no optional child1 G on this reentry.

Consequently, conditional on a completed B at a permanently entered root, its number of G fingers is EXACTLY the ensuing spine exponent. Also a B at that root and an optional G during the ensuing exponent-two stage cannot both occur. This does not constrain an unpreceded stage's optional G, nor the integer depths of the B's one or two fingers.

For an eastward exponent-two block, the two apparent north-finger sources in the column pair {2k,2k+1} are the mandatory G_L in a B_R at w_k and the optional G_L at u_k in the same parent's ensuing R stage. They are mutually exclusive by this lemma. The analogous south pair {2k+1,2k+2} has at most one of the mandatory G_R in B_L at l_k and optional G_R at v_k. These are the exact stage groupings used in the transverse-mask refinement.
