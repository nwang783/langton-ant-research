## 1. Criterion for an actual canonical child

A canonical edge H -> J is selected at rank1 of its physical contact. It has the negative flat pattern R0,L1 arriving with the SAME heading and R2 with the opposite heading: R0 chooses H and L1 switches H -> J. The canonical birth of J is that L1. Thus an arbitrary first-L switch is not enough to make J a canonical child. A lower-height target cannot be a maximum child, and a target already touched by that contact's R0 cannot be born at its later L1. Optional rank3 never supplies a second canonical child at the same contact.

In particular the canonical incoming contact of a maximum face cannot also carry one of its outgoing canonical children. Each maximum face has at most THREE canonical children, one at each other physical corner. This statement is independent of K1.
