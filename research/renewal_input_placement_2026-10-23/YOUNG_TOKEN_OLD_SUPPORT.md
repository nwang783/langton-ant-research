## 2. An intrinsic full-state cycle-age statistic

For a finite black graph G with actual lifetime birth b(f) on every edge,
let e=uv be a nonbridge. Define

    mu_G(e) = max over simple cycles C containing e of min_{f in C} b(f).

Equivalently, let lambda be the maximum, over u-to-v paths in G-e, of the
minimum birth on that path. Then

    mu_G(e) = min(b(e), lambda).

Indeed a simple bypass plus e is a simple cycle, and deleting e from any
simple cycle gives a simple bypass. A finite walk can have loops removed
without lowering its bottleneck. This equivalence is exact and does not
select a convenient but potentially misleading cycle witness. For any
threshold a, mu_G(e)>a if and only if e belongs to a cycle in the subgraph
of edges whose birth is >a. Thus a component closure at a threshold can
certify an upper bound using the COMPLETE graph, even when most of that
graph is not explicitly listed.

The present theorem proves mu_G(q)=beta_N. The relevant old support is not
just an arbitrarily chosen witness: every possible replacement cycle is
charged. This statistic is not asserted to be monotone under ant updates.

## 6. Scope of the organization lesson

The older placement theorem in PROOF.md distinguishes standard104 from the
1564/4352 words by consumed lifetime placement. The present result identifies
a further variable that cannot be dropped when building a causal renewal
graph: the support lifetimes of a nonbridge deletion, including untouched
archives, can be much older than its deleted token. It does not supply the
missing global control on how such archives are repeatedly produced,
transported, and used. The family also shows that a bounded consumed-token
age alone does not turn the old-input placement result into a global fresh
self-feeding versus old-token dichotomy.
