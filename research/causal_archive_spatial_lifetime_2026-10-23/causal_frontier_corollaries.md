## 1. Linear C-clock spatial nonreturn

For any two actual atomic cuts s<t in one simple corrected-center factor,
let z_s,z_t be their actual corrected centers. The signed thin-chord theorem
implies

    C_t-C_s <= 20*||z_t-z_s||_infinity +111.

If the centers coincide, injectivity makes the subarc empty, which satisfies
the displayed inequality. Equivalently

    ||z_t-z_s||_infinity >= (C_t-C_s-111)/20.

Thus every fixed spatial region of L-infinity diameterD has C-clock span at
most20D+111 between any two of its visits. This is a quantitative causal
reuse window, not a bound on fine duration between those visits. Arbitrarily
many zero-C atoms cannot be charged as C increments.

## 2. Linear spatial horizon for untouched entry identities

c=(-1/2,-1/2). Suppose every residual identity present at that entry has cell

    C_consumer <=20(R+5)+111 =20R+211.

## 3. Sealing-time horizon for produced-boundary archives

Suppose at a full packet boundary of clock s a white-dual archive is frozen
away from the current coupled w cursor, with every black boundary edge a
current produced residual identity sourced inside the same factor. Every
such source clock is at most s. If this archive remains frozen through a
later full boundary of clock greater than s+294, every one of those boundary
identities is then older than294 and cannot occur in any later complete
context. No later physical L gate can reload that archive in this factor.

The archive need not be represented by a single simple Jordan boundary;
its first reload still requires an actual L read of a boundary black cell.
If one uses an explicit pure fence instead, the existing coupled-w Jordan
shield theorem applies unchanged. Entry identities, old sources outside the
factor, core boundary edges, or merely historical incidence connections do
not qualify. The clock statement concerns full boundaries and does not
identify an ordered input retirement with its later physical L deletion.
