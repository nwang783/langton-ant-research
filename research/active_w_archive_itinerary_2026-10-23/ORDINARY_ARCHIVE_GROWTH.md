# Temporary ordinary reattachments grow a protected full archive

24 October2026. Author global scoped lemma, frozen for independent audit.
This is an exact complete-field consequence of old-bridge inheritance, not
a bound across arbitrary exceptional renewals or an initial-root budget.

## 1. Setting and statement

Consider a proper post-L cut, followed by complete ordinary blocks R^r L,
where r<=2 and every L is a w BRIDGE deletion. An incomplete terminal R
prefix is excluded from the post-L statements. Let F_0 be the COMPLETE entry
active w component, and let D_0 be ANY different complete black component,
with d>=1 edges. Choose a surviving lifetime e in D_0 as an archive marker.
All fields, including other distant components, are retained.

By the signed old-bridge theorem, the post-L active components F_j form
exact nested old far-side components of F_0. Every consumed L edge belongs
to that old entry path; every new R arm survives and is left on the departed
side. The marker e is therefore never consumed during this ordinary interval.

Let D_j be the COMPLETE post-L component containing e. Then:

1. D_0 subset D_1 subset D_2 subset ... as ACTUAL EDGE-LIFETIME sets.
   No old edge of D_j is deleted or disconnected at any later post-L cut.
2. A temporary reattachment of D_{j-1} can occur only in an R prefix; there
   is at most one dormant-to-active reactivation of e in a block. Its following
   bridge L leaves e dormant again.
3. If block j reattaches that archive, ALL r_j new R arms of that block
   belong to D_j and remain in every later D_k. They are distinct current
   R-born lifetimes, not repeatedly charged coordinates.

Let W be the sum of r_j over blocks that temporarily reattach e, and let k
be their number. Then W>=k and the final archive contains at least d+W edges.
Moreover, just before the L of the LAST such block, the complete ACTIVE
component has at least

    d+W+1

edges. The extra1 is its old departing bridge, distinct from D_0 and all
charged R arms. Thus if the complete active w component has at most M edges
at EVERY fine cut, then

    W <= max(0,M-d-1),       k <= max(0,M-d-1).

For an arbitrary lifetime first left dormant later in an ordinary epoch,
restart at that post-L cut. The same protected-archive statement applies.
No dormant interval or component is assumed to be LIFO.

## 2. Proof with full components

At the entry cut D_0 is outside F_0. The old-bridge inheritance theorem says
that each future L crosses the next old bridge in its retained far-side
component. Any R arm from that block is on the DEPARTED side of that bridge,
even if it imports another entire field or creates cycles behind the bridge.
A connection to the old far side that bypassed the departing bridge would
make that L nonbridge and leave this ordinary scope.

Inductively every edge of D_0, and every archive edge retained at a previous
post-L cut, is outside the current far-side F_j. No future ordinary L can
delete any of those edges, because all such L edges lie in the retained old
far-side component. Their existing connecting paths remain intact. R can
only add edges or merge complete fields. Therefore their complete post-L
component cannot shrink.

If an R prefix reaches D_{j-1}, it imports that FULL component into the
stationary w cursor's active component. R insertion cannot deactivate e, so
there is at most one reactivation within that prefix. All R arms are incident
to the same cursor. At the following ordinary bridge L, those arms and the
imported archive remain on the departed side together. Every r_j arm is
therefore attached to D_{j-1} and survives there.

Different ordinary R writes are distinct lifetimes and even distinct physical
cells during the interval: an R-written cell cannot be R-written again unless
its black lifetime is consumed, whereas no ordinary L consumes an R-created
arm. This proves the cumulative d+W protected edge count.

Immediately before the last relevant L, the active component contains the
original D_0, every previously charged R arm, and all current block R arms.
It also contains its departing old bridge f. That f belongs to the entry
old path, so it is neither in D_0 nor one of the new arms. This proves d+W+1.
For k=0 the charge is zero; for k>0 the fine-cut cap M gives the displayed
inequality. A post-L-only cap would NOT suffice: nearly all the charged
archive is dormant again at that cut. QED.

## 3. An actual sharp fine-cut control

The independently signed joined detoured collector family from
`../actual_zipper_spiral_2026-10-23/one_component_paired_forest_collector.md`
provides a concrete sharp instance. The q8,spacing8 member has351 initial
black edges in one full active tree. Its first bridge L leaves a dormant
collector archive of d=191 edges. Over the rest of the same ordinary epoch,
a marker in that archive is temporarily reattached159 times. The reattaching
blocks write a total W=166 R arms. Every one remains in that SAME complete
archive, whose final mass is357. The last pre-L active mass is358, attaining

    M=d+W+1=191+166+1.

Both black graphs stay forests in this entire epoch. The count is not caused
by hidden cycle erasures, an infinite prefunded rail, or uncharged exterior
input. All351 original cells are explicitly present and charged. This actual
control shows why one cannot replace the growth ledger by a constant bound
on temporary reattachments without also bounding fine-cut active mass.

`check_active_itineraries.py` and `active_itinerary_controls.json` reconstruct
this full field and every protected post-L archive. The same checker verifies

## 4. Connection to the attraction gap

The signed ACTIVE_EPISODES.md trilemma says a non-eventually-translating
trajectory must sustain active-component growth, long dormant gaps, or
arbitrarily many bridge-departure/R-reimport episodes of consumed R lifetimes.
The present lemma adds a real stateful restriction to the third branch:
within ONE ordinary epoch, repeated temporary returns grow a protected
complete archive and force large active components at R-prefix fine cuts.

It does not control visits separated by w-exceptional renewals. Once a
renewal makes an old archive the next active entry field, later ordinary
bridge deletions can consume and split that field; the protected post-L
monotonicity cannot simply be carried through that change of role. The
serial-release/splice theorem retains the resulting full blocks and ports,
but currently supplies no uniform cross-renewal budget. This is the precise
remaining place where a sustained mechanism is needed.
