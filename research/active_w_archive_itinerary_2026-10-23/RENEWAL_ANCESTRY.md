# Entry-active mass and renewal-clock ancestry control physical lifetime age

24 October2026. Author sufficient condition for arbitrary actual finite ants,
frozen for independent audit. This is a new organization of retained exact

No sustained mass or ancestry-lag bound is asserted to follow from finite input.

## 1. A renewal clock based on actual full w state

Use proper compressed blocks R^r L. They are ordinary when r<=2 and the L
edge is a bridge in the COMPLETE current w graph. Every other block is a
genuine w-exceptional renewal: in a proper block r=4 or L is w-nonbridge.
An initial partial block may have r=3; the same exceptional convention covers
it, and one finite initial block can always be discarded.

Let tau_i be successive exceptional L update times and s_i=tau_i+1 their
actual post-L cuts. Write

    c_i = |C_(s_i)|,

where C is the COMPLETE entry active w component. All dormant archives and
both physical cursors are retained. There is no assumption that c_i equals
complete black mass, includes the emitted wake, or decreases with i.

**Entry-active clock lemma.** A next exceptional L always exists, and

    s_(i+1)-s_i <= 3c_i+5.

**Proof.** Exact old-bridge inheritance makes every ordinary L before the
next exceptional block consume a distinct edge of the ENTRY ACTIVE field,
not an arbitrary initial black edge. Its post-L component is the exact old
far-side component. Therefore there are at most c_i complete ordinary blocks,
each of length at most3. An R-only prefix cannot continue indefinitely: at
most four R occur before the next L. The next exceptional block has length
at most5. This gives the bound and existence. The complete fields temporarily
imported at R prefixes are left behind by their ordinary bridge L; their
possibly huge masses do not lengthen a block. QED.

This is the retained zipper time bound sharpened from full board mass to
actual ENTRY ACTIVE mass, as already supported by the exact ordinary-epoch
input ledger. Even a cap only on the post-renewal c_i, not on every R-prefix
active mass, suffices for this temporal lemma.

## 2. Token ancestry in that renewal clock

Let nu(t) be the number of exceptional L updates STRICTLY BEFORE cut t.
For an actual consumed R-born lifetime, let b be its R update and d its
consuming L update. Define its renewal age by

    j(e)=nu(d+1)-nu(b).

This counts actual exceptional L updates in the inclusive interval[b,d].
It uses the current lifetime, so deletion and rewriting at one coordinate
do not reuse a birth. It does not count ancestry roots or merely cycles
chosen as witnesses.

The lifetime interval intersects at most j(e)+1 renewal epochs. Hence the
following GLOBAL weighted inequality holds, using the ACTUAL entry fields:

    d-b <= sum_(epochs intersecting [b,d]) (3c_i+5).

An initial partial epoch can be included with its own finite entry-active
mass or removed by beginning after the first exceptional L. This inequality
is not a descent law: the actual c_i may grow, and a token can cross many
epochs. It is a precise relation between actual parent-lifetime lag and
actual full-state input sizes.

## 3. A sufficient sustained ancestry condition

Suppose that after some post-renewal cut T:

- every subsequent renewal entry has c_i<=M;
- every R-born lifetime born after T and eventually consumed has renewal
  age j(e)<=J.

Then all those lifetimes have physical age at most

    D=(J+1)(3M+5).

There are only finitely many lifetimes already black at T, and each can be
consumed at most once. After the last future consumption from that finite
set, EVERY consumed black lifetime has age<=D.

Apply the RETAINED bounded-consumed-lifetime theorem in
`../sources/kehao95-core/memory_feedback/proof/stale-memory.md`, Sections1--4:
absence of the stale-black predicate S_D makes the actual D-letter suffix
predictor autonomous, so the trajectory is eventually a NONZERO TRANSLATING
PERIODIC TRACE. This implication is not being rediscovered here. No claim
that every finite-seed translator is standard104 is made.

The hypotheses concern a sustained complete-field state property and actual
token-parent lag in a renewal clock. They are stronger than an instantaneous
small component and different from merely bounding a deleted token's old
cycle-support witness. Unread topology can alter graph segmentation without
supplying execution fuel; only actual birth/death lifetimes enter j(e).

## 4. What failure of eventual translation would require

For a trajectory with no eventual translating periodic trace, the retained
stale-memory result forces unbounded consumed ages in EVERY tail. Excluding
the finitely many current black lifetimes at a checkpoint leaves unbounded
ages among consumed R lifetimes BORN after it.

Consequently, on every such trajectory, either renewal-entry active masses
are unbounded in every tail, or—if those masses are eventually bounded—the
actual renewal ages j(e) of late-born consumed tokens are unbounded. The
alternative is not asserted exclusive. A finite original root set does not
bound these lags: new R-born outputs can be archived and later promoted into
new entry fields.

This is the attraction gap in renewal-ancestry terms. The serial archive
calculus and ORDINARY_ARCHIVE_GROWTH.md constrain reattachments inside an
ordinary phase, but do not yet bound how often an old archive can change
roles across exceptional renewals, then be consumed as fresh epoch-entry
stock. The fixed184 finite waves show why a bound uniform in original mass
alone is unavailable. They do not realize one infinite counterexample, and
no argument here proves the stated sustained hypotheses eventually occur.

## 5. Relation to the active-episode condition

ACTIVE_EPISODES.md provides a complementary decomposition: a fixed lifetime
cannot remain continuously active in uniformly small components for too long;
its other elapsed time is literal bridge-departure/R-reimport dormancy.
ORDINARY_ARCHIVE_GROWTH.md proves repeated temporary returns within one
ordinary phase add protected current R arms to the archived field and force
large active fine cuts.

The present renewal-clock version bypasses a separate bound on each dormant
gap: when entry masses are bounded, long gaps must contain many exceptional
renewals. Bounding actual token lag in that clock would therefore suffice.
It remains a substantive unproved condition, not a global finite-state
quotient, not a finite summary of arbitrary archives, and not an attraction
theorem in disguise.
