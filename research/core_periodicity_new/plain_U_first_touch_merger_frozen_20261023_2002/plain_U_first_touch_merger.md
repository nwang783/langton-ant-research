# A reset plain-U component must import an outside component at its first touch

Inside an actual complete recurrent-eight factor, reset the residual genealogy immediately before a genuine plain U packet starts. The U consumes five root identities and creates a component C consisting exactly of its nine fresh outputs. If a later full packet ever consumes one of those nine identities, the first such packet must also consume an identity from an outside component. Thus C cannot begin an isolated all-component helper evolution.

This is explicitly a reset statement. If the five original U inputs already belong to a larger historical component containing other residual identities, those extra identities are not covered. It is not the open general U historical-component-merger theorem. UC and UCC source components are also not included here.

## Proof using the audited first-helper gate

Normalize the creating U to (0,0,N), source start C clock zero. Suppose its first later touch P consumes only C identities. All nine creating-U outputs remain the same current identities until P. The independently checked first-helper theorem forces

- P = D or DC at (2,3,W), consuming only the old exit bud (1,3);
- P starts at C clock exactly five.

P is not the immediate successor of the creating U. Let F be that immediate full successor, starting at (0,4,N).

If F is U, UC, or UCC, its fresh permanent left star is {(0,4),(0,5),(1,4),(1,5)}. The complete context of either possible P meets this star, so P is impossible.

Otherwise F is D, DC, V, VC, or VCC. Every such F has the genuine fresh full-output guard z=(2,4). Its internal C tail does not read z. The guard lies in P's active core, not among P's inputs, so its residual identity must retire strictly before P. A current residual identity cannot simply become an active-core cell.

Enumerate all 92 consumer/input-slot/heading alignments for z for each F and each possible P. Hold all nine old creating-U outputs current. Reject wrong sectors, complete-context contacts with those unchanged identities, and forbidden contacts with the past creating U, past F, or future P atomic chords. The exact immediate-adjacency exceptions are retained rather than discarded.

Of 920 alignments, 460 have wrong sector, 350 meet an unchanged creating-U output, 20 meet a forbidden past chord, 33 meet a forbidden future-P chord, and 57 geometrically surviving rows remain. Every survivor is W headed, has entry y equal to four or five, and has full exit y at least five. All such rows are recorded.

Let k be the guard consumer's start C clock. Heading W forces k=1 modulo four. Since it occurs before P at clock five, only k=1 or k=5 is possible.

- Clock one is impossible: after the creating U exits at (0,4,N), a prefix with exactly one C has only N-oriented zero-C packets, then one DC/VC/UC turning packet, then W-oriented zero-C packets. The N zero packets strictly increase y, the turning packet increases y by at least two, and the W zero packets never decrease y. Hence every clock-one full-packet entry has y at least six, not four or five.
- At clock five, a positive-C guard consumer would finish after P's clock and so is impossible. A zero-C guard consumer finishes W headed with y at least five. Any subsequent clock-five packets have zero C and remain W headed; D, V, and U have W-oriented y increments one, one, and zero. They cannot reach P's entry y=3.

Thus the guard cannot retire before P. This contradicts the requirement that z be in P's active core. Both branches for F are impossible, proving that the first touch of C must genuinely import an outside component.

The endpoint inequalities handle arbitrarily long zero-C runs. The checker performs no trajectory, prefix-depth, or box search. No global bound on the number of U births follows: arbitrary historical components, source C tails, and post-merger evolution remain unresolved.
