## Exhaustive literal and center proof

The goal's entry core contains r, so its old residual identity must retire
strictly before that entry. Every retirement is through a true input of a
complete packet. Placing each of the 23 retained input slots at r in each of
four headings gives92 cases, without a spatial or word-length bound.

Forty-six placements have the wrong conserved sector. Forty more touch one
of P in their literal COMPLETE raw-plus-marker contexts; they cannot retire
r while all five exact P identities survive. The common M11 itself misses
all five future U inputs, so this argument remains valid even for a packet
immediately before a terminal goal with only its actual marker.

Only six placements remain. Three are U/UC/UCC at(-2,-1,E). Their U-base
center chord goes from(-5/2,-1/2) to(3/2,-1/2), passing strictly through the
future goal ENTRY center(-1/2,-1/2). This is an earlier nonadjacent repeat,
contradicting simplicity regardless of the consumer's later C tails.

The other three are exactly the plain D/V/U packets displayed above. Each
ends at the exact goal pose(0,0,N). Its last atomic endpoint is the future
goal's entry center. If ANY atom intervened between this exit and the goal,
that center would occur twice at distinct path times. Thus this retirement
packet must be the actual immediate predecessor, not an unspecified earlier
support packet. Consecutive shared endpoints are properly allowed.

The V and U helper options consume BOTH old core spares together. The D
option consumes only r, so s must have retired earlier. No impossibility of
these alternatives is asserted. In particular, one may not charge two core
spares as two separate cleanup trips. The q and four siblings may already
share an older historical incidence component, and this theorem does not
infer a new root or a component merger.

The general lemma needs only simplicity of the stated actual factor. Signed
U-entry simplicity supplies that condition when the factor begins U-family;
it does not make arbitrary later factors or arbitrary atomic words simple.
