## Theorem

Take one directed U-cycle inheritance chain inside a physically actual complete recurrent-eight history. Reset the factor at the chain's first creating U/UC/UCC entry. Normalize that head to (0,0,N), with C clock zero. Give every residual identity present there its own entry root, including remote unread debris. Define

    M = maximum x-coordinate of those finitely many entry residual roots.

Then M>=0. If M<3, this chain has no renewal. Otherwise it has at most 3M+5 renewal links, hence at most 3M+6 U-family events. For an arbitrary creating pose, use the corresponding translated rightward coordinate in its heading frame.

In particular, no single such chain can be infinite on a finite entry board. This bound depends on spatial entry data, not just entry mass.

    j <= 3M+5.

## Consequence for a hypothetical infinite retained history

Every inheritance chain is finite. If there are infinitely many U events, there must therefore be infinitely many separate chain births. The final U in each chain has an output unit cycle that is never first-broken later in the retained history, so its four exact residual identities remain forever current. Distinct terminal cycles have disjoint physical cell sets: later fresh output cannot overlap an old unretired residual identity.

Thus an infinite complete-eight U-entry history would need infinitely many distinct permanent U unit cycles. This is a new necessary topology, not a contradiction. No bound on the number of new chains, no initial-root charge for dormant unit-face islands, and no historical-component separation has been proved here.
