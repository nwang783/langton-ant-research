## Exact produced-only reduction

A still-current entry identity has never been consumed. Therefore it has
never met a packet vertex and remains a singleton historical component,
with no other current identity in that component. Hence a U event with all
five inputs in ONE component necessarily has ALL FIVE inputs produced
inside the factor. The converse is false: produced inputs may belong to
several historical components.

For a U-entry factor the signed young-age-three boundary theorem excludes
produced lower age three: its producer would have to be the factor-first

at least one C. These are necessary chronology constraints, not an exclusion
of the candidate monochromatic U. No new age inventory is proposed here.

## Missing topological premise, not an assumed planar graph

An arbitrary connected planar tree can join the five U input points while
avoiding its center chord, by passing above the chord's endpoint. Historical
incidence edges can run through cells which are now white; CURRENT live
component archives need not remain visibly connected. Packet D transports
its input(0,1) to output(2,0), and a connecting arc can pass around its center
endpoint without crossing the D chord. These facts block naive statements
that abstract incidence connectivity itself must intersect the center path.

A valid proof needs an exact causal embedding, a current-core ribbon/escape
gate invariant, or another physical relation binding historical component
connections to the actual past transport. The before-white U context and
ordered token survival are available constraints, but no maintained spatial
embedding has been proved. The first obligation is adversarially to test
that premise, not to declare historical edges planar by fiat.
