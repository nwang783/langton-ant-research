# Three retained U remnants keep the old white-gate output alive

Normalize an actual creating U/UC/UCC to(0,0,N). Let W be its original west cycle pair at(1,0),(1,1), and let r be its original output(0,0). As long as all three identities W+r remain current and survive, its original output w=(0,1) cannot retire.

Crucially, the original four-cycle need NOT remain intact. Its east pair may already have been consumed by an earlier right first breaker. This is not an application of the left-FIRST-break exclusion to a later west-pair consumer.

Proof: align every retained true-input consumer with w. Of92 alignments,46 have wrong sector and40 have complete contexts touching protected W+r. The six remaining placements are D/DC(0,2,S), V/VC(0,4,S), and U/UC(-1,5,S). Every BASE exits at doubled corrected center(-1,3), the strict midpoint of the creating U-base chord(-1,-1)--(-1,7). A suffix beginning at the creating U has injective center by signed U-entry simplicity, so all six are impossible. This is an interior contact, not a permissible shared adjacent endpoint; even a consumer immediately after a source UCC cannot avoid the older source-U-base contact. Thus w has no first retirement while those three identities survive. No age or bounded history is assumed.

A later U/UC/UCC at(0,2,S) would consume exactly W+r as its lower a,q and upper c, but requires w before-white. Hence that retained three-cohort transfer is impossible, even after a prior right renewal. In normalized goal coordinates, this excludes sourceU/UC/UCC(0,2,S) supplying a,q,c.

Different source producers may still belong to one historical component. This theorem does not prohibit every later west-pair use, only a use that also retains this r identity, and does not bound U events or prove confinement.
