## Statement and exact language scope

Split each actual retained packet into its D, V or U base followed by zero,
one or two C atoms. Consider any of the resulting ACTUAL marked cuts inside
a valid complete recurrent-eight factor, including cuts inside VCC or UCC.
No such cut can return to exactly the same head and heading after FOUR C
increments. The source may be the factor's first cut.

This concerns atomic cuts arising from a real complete-eight factor. It does
not license an arbitrary word over the enlarged alphabet D, V, U, C, nor extend
protected-source clauses to unsupported standalone-C histories.

## Literal common output at all atomic sources

The signed literal C reconstruction gives no residual inputs and exactly three
fresh residual outputs: (1, 2), (2, 0), (2, 1). Its raw endpoint is (0, 1, W).
D, V and U also have the fresh entry-relative output (2, 0). The output is
initially white at the common marker's read 8 and outside the entry core.
Thus every possible atomic source writes a genuine fresh identity at (2, 0),
not an entry-exempt identity. C preserves all older residual identities.

At each atomic source the fresh identity needs at least three atomic
source-included C increments before its first consuming base. This clock claim
has TWO separately justified cases:

1. A D, V or U base, or the FIRST C of a full packet, starts at the full source's
   birth clock. Ordinary JO input minima therefore apply without a shift.
   Ages zero and one are impossible. At age two only a V lower input could
   qualify, but its required relative pose (2, 3, S) has the wrong sector.
2. The SECOND C starts one increment after the full producer's birth clock.
   Its atomic local output (2, 0) is full VCC output (1, 6), or full UCC output
   (0, 7). Signed 1340 checks all 184 full-source query alignments at ages zero
   through three: 92 fail sector and 92 are already signed counted exclusions.
   Atomic age is full age minus one. Thus atomic ages below three are excluded
   here too. No input can consume it within a remaining source C tail, because
   C consumes no residuals.

The second case is essential. Ordinary JO full-source ages must NOT simply be
reused as atomic ages across that one-increment birth shift.

## The same geometric endpoint proof now applies

Normalize an atomic source to (0, 0, N), and let t be its fresh output (2, 0).
All residual-consuming atoms are D, V and U; standalone C has no residual input.
At an exact four-C later cut, a first retirement of t can occur only at atomic
age three or four.

At age three the consuming base is oriented E. Every input/base placement ends
at x >= 2. Exactly one C remains before the later cut; the E-to-N C adds +1 in
x, while every zero-C E or N base has nonnegative x displacement. The later
head would have x >= 3.

At age four the consuming base is oriented N. Every input/base placement ends
at x >= 2, and subsequent zero-C N motion cannot move left. The later head
would have x >= 2.

This argument is made at ATOMIC cuts. A full consuming packet may continue
past the target cut with further C tails; that does not invalidate the proof.
Only the actual C atoms before the target are counted. In particular, a
retiring E base at age three may be followed by the FIRST C of a two-C tail,
with the target cut between the tails. The one remaining C displacement is
still exactly the one used above. No full-packet completion assumption is
silently carried into the atomic proof.

All sixteen endpoint inequalities are the signed 1333 inequalities, now with
correctly justified atomic maturity. Hence t remains current before every
four-C later cut whose head has x <= 1. In an exact pose return it would be
black at (2, 0), whereas the target's ACTUAL common marker requires that cell
white at read 8. This is a literal contradiction and proves the statement.
