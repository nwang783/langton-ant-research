# Audit of the proposed hyperbolic-stack route

22 October 2026. Status: exact finite counterexample candidate to a published auxiliary bound; **not** a highway-conjecture counterexample or a proof of boundary convergence.

## What was going to be transferred

Gajardo, *A symbolic projection of Langton's Ant (Extended Abstract)*, DMTCS AB(DMCS), 2003, pp.57–68, [official PDF](https://dmtcs.episciences.org/2312/pdf), page 65:

* Theorem 2(2) states that every cell is visited at most four times for the ant on regular k-face/d-valence graphs when d≥5,k≥4 (or d≥6,k=3).
* Theorem 3(5) states H44⊂H45 for the read-word languages.
* The arc group includes the relators R⁴=L⁴=1.

If all those statements could be applied as written, every square-ant trace would have a hyperbolic realization and each state of its reduced-prefix walk in C4*C4 would occur at most four times. A locally finite tree-of-squares argument would then make the reduced walk proper and give a unique limiting normal-form ray. This would have been a useful non-recurrent ledger for projected-site collisions.

The finite audit below blocks that transfer. Do not use the four-visit bound or the proposed boundary conclusion in any proof.

## Exact square witness

Let the turn word be

    RRLLLLRLLLLRLLLLRRRRLRRLLLLRRLRRRRLLL

It has 37 symbols. From pose (0,0), north, it is generated by the finite black set

    {(-1,-1),(-1,0),(-1,2),(0,-2),(0,-1),(0,1),(0,2),
     (1,-2),(1,-1),(2,-1),(2,0)}.

All other cells can be white. It also occurs at times 2924 through 2960 inclusive in the usual blank-start square ant under this convention.

The origin is read at times 0,12,16,20,28,36, with turns R,L,R,L,R,L. The reduced prefix in C4*C4 equals the empty word at times 0,12,16,20,36; at time28 its normal form is LRRR. Thus one reduced-prefix state occurs five times. This already prevents the proposed *square-trace* consequence of the quoted source claims.

## Independent exact hyperbolic check

To check that this was not merely an invalid transfer of square visits into lifted visits, construct the regular {4,5} tessellation in the Poincaré disk. Let

    r = sqrt(cos(2π/5)) = sqrt(sqrt(5)-1)/2,
    S(z) = exp(2πi/5) z,
    A(z) = (r-z)/(1-rz).

Here S rotates by one of the five edges at the vertex 0, and A is the half-turn about the midpoint of the edge 0→r. The edge length satisfies cosh(length/2)=cos(π/4)/sin(π/5), the regular {4,5} relation. Represent a directed edge by g(0)→g(r). Its two nearest-edge continuations are gAS and gAS⁻¹. These label R and L, up to an irrelevant global exchange of handedness.

The checker represents all matrices over the exact degree-16 algebraic number field generated by i, sqrt(5), sqrt(10−2sqrt(5)), and sqrt(sqrt(5)−1). It verifies A²,S⁵,(AS)⁴,(AS⁻¹)⁴ are projectively the identity. It compares two visited points by exact cross multiplication in that field, without floating-point tolerances.

The exact visited-site IDs, assigned on first occurrence, are:

    0,1,2,3,4,1,2,5,6,7,2,5,0,8,9,5,0,1,2,5,
    0,8,10,11,12,13,10,11,0,8,9,14,15,8,9,5,0.

The initially black IDs are

    2,3,4,5,6,7,8,9,11,12,13.

At every repeated ID, consecutive symbols of the word alternate. Therefore these initial colors really generate the finite trace; all unvisited vertices may be white. In particular hyperbolic site0 is read six times, at the same times listed above. This is not a bare numerical plot or a test of only the R⁴/L⁴ relations.

## Reproduction and remaining review boundary

Run `python audit_symbolic_lift.py` with Python and SymPy 1.14. The independently written checker emits `symbolic_lift_witness.json`, including every square pose and normal form, all exact hyperbolic equality classes, and the initial colors. It imports no code from the ant repositories.

The finite computations are exact. The remaining interpretative review is matching the tessellation and nearest-edge convention to the cited paper and checking whether an omitted hypothesis or correction exists elsewhere. The paper explicitly defines k as face size and d as vertex degree, and defines L/R by the nearest adjacent edge on the chosen side. The displayed model appears to match. Nevertheless, the research map labels this an audit flag rather than silently declaring the entire paper invalid. The elementary alternating-visit trace criterion and unrelated literature results are unaffected.

## What survives as a useful direction

The free-product stack is still an exact, cheap descriptor of any turn word. A different finite upper bound on repeated stack states, if proved independently, would still yield properness and a limiting ray. It must not be inferred from the four-visit statement. Moreover, even a correct boundary theorem would not prove the finite-support highway conjecture: finite support says that only finitely many **projected square sites** are first read L, and generally does not say that only finitely many lifted sites are first read L. The missing theorem must control those projection fibers or charge their parity interactions.

There is a sharp conditional obstruction. Theorem 5 of Gajardo–Goles–Moreira, [STACS 2001](https://www.dim.uchile.cl/~anmoreir/oficial/langton_stacs.pdf), states that a finite-support ant on regular {4,5} eventually has period 15. If that theorem is used as stated, any realization on {4,5} of the standard nonconstant 104-periodic square-ant tail must have infinitely many initially L sites. Otherwise its turn tail would have periods 104 and 15, hence period gcd(104,15)=1, a contradiction. This is an elementary deduction from that source theorem, not an independent reconstruction of its proof. It explains why simply proving finite support of the lift cannot be the missing lemma: it would exclude the desired solution itself.
