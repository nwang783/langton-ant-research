## A genuine r/B pair cannot disappear late after one foreign

Assume both SAME identities are CURRENT before the FIRST foreign and its COMPLETE RAW plus actual M11 context MISSES both. If the true source-to-foreign C age is at least three, a later first CURRENT loss of either identity forces a proxy contact by completion of that loss atom. Consequently both remain current at ENTRY of a global first-contact atom.

This is a geometric earliest-loss lemma, not an old input-age theorem reused after the foreign. No original A survival is assumed.

Choose the earliest loss of r or B under an otherwise simple proxy. The signed ordinary r gate inventory has only D(1,0,W), V(3,0,W), U(4,1,W). Each requires B already absent before that atom, either WHITE or in its ENTRY CORE, and none takes B as a true input. Thus r cannot be the first loss, and simultaneous loss is impossible as well. This uses literal before-context facts, not an arbitrary ordering of two physical Ls inside a RAW.

The actual source V/C tails meet it only at E. The consuming base prefix meets it only at Z; for the lower-q U this is its midpoint. While r and B are held, all prior ordinary and repaired-foreign proxy pieces avoid this bridge. If the loss atom is completed before the global first contact, the source-to-Z arc is simple and the bridge closes a genuine Jordan curve.

Let original C=(2,1) be CURRENT at the start of a zero-C W run, with SAME B held before each considered atom. Its eight plain input alignments give six wrong sectors and two U cases. Every further zero-C W base decreases x. Hence at a later cut with head x>=3, the SAME bud could not already have been consumed. This endpoint guard needs no simplicity or postforeign age law.
