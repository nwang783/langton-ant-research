The signed bounded-active frozen-excursion theorem says: for ONE actual finite-seed orbit, if there exist T,M,A such that every fine active w component after T has at most M edges and every completed dormant interval of every late-born consumed lifetime has at most A cuts, then actual consumed ages are eventually bounded. The signed finite-source erasure theorem says: for each supplied M there exists a finite F(M) bounding a completed marked departure-to-first-read lag IF no lifetime already black at that departure is consumed outside the original4M box. It permits all later R writing. Deletion preserves the exact physical trace and complete marked component; M must remain the ORIGINAL parameter.

Neither statement removes its capitalized hypothesis. In particular bounded component size is not a bound on the positions, waiting times, or future import schedule of passive components. The exact active-flight procedure retains that full field and stops at its first real import; it is not a finite-state replacement for an unbounded import itinerary.

F(M) is the maximum of the finite FIRST-HIT times among a finite set of complete boxed input states. This establishes existence, not a computable stopping test. Counting the boxed INPUT states does not bound those times by the number of states inside the box: the new orbit may write and travel arbitrarily outside that initial box. No uncomputability conclusion is justified either.

The signed helper relation gives interleaving s<d<=j<c, decreasing departures and deaths along a backward arrow. Sharing is one obstacle to charging helpers, but eliminating sharing alone would not close the proof.

    d_n < d_(n+1) <= c_n < c_(n+1),
