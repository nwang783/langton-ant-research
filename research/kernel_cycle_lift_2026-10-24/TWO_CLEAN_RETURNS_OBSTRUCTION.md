## 2. The physical two-return obstruction

**Theorem.** None of AA, AC, CA or CC has a complete-board physical lift with
the prescribed full active-component paths. This holds for arbitrary finite
unread exterior fields; no passive component can be moved, erased or supplied
between updates. Both single blocks do have the actual finite-seed controls
above.

**Proof for A followed by A or C.** At the empty cut after A, its full old
three-edge star S is dormant and unchanged. At an empty cursor all four ports
are white, so the next four updates are necessarily R, with stationary w.
Their new edges occupy all four ports. The fourth R reconnects to S's center.
Until this first contact no L could alter S, and any earlier exterior import
would already violate the stipulated next clean path. At the fourth R there
are three prior new active edges, the new connecting edge and all three S
edges: active mass7. Thus neither prescribed clean cap-five path can continue.
This is the known blank-reset flight collision applied to the precise A path,
not a newly discovered independent reset phenomenon.

**Proof for C followed by A or C.** Its new singleton edge E--(E+N) stays
literally frozen after C. The next empty cursor is2E, and its next first-R
direction is N. The old singleton touches its NONforward neighbor E. The
next four mandatory R updates sweep all cursor ports, so their fourth R
imports that entire old singleton. It is NOT imported at the first R.

This is incompatible with A, which has no imports, and with C, whose only
import is a singleton at its FIRST R. Other unread black cells cannot remove
the compulsory old singleton: touching it earlier would itself be a differing
import, and there is no L among these four R updates. With the literal minimal
C board, active masses after the four R updates are1,2,3,5. Thus this is a
WRONG-PORT identity obstruction, not necessarily an immediate cap failure.
The turn word can still hold while the full component/kernel path differs.
All four pair cases are now excluded. QED.

This rules out arbitrary mixtures of the two clean paths after even two

## 3. Exact full-component compatibility test, with finite witnesses

The checker also supplies a general exact FINITE supplied-profile lift test.
At cut t let the proposed complete active component be C_t and cursor w_t.
Take all physical ports incident to vertices of C_t OR w_t (including the
isolated cursor when C_t is empty). Require precisely C_t to be black among
these ports and every other one white. Since C_t is connected to w_t, these
requirements are equivalent to its being the COMPLETE active component;
outside edges cannot join it without violating a boundary-port requirement.

For each such port q, pull the required bit back to the initial board using

    required initial bit(q)
       = required cut-t bit(q) XOR parity(reads of q before t).

The read positions are the actual supplied turn plan. Inconsistent pulled-
back bits forbid EVERY initial board, including arbitrary unread extras. If
they are consistent, the finite set of ports forced initially black, with all
others white, constructs a literal finite board realizing every supplied
full component and turn, by first-difference induction. This test retains
physical archive placement and actual toggles, rather than matching passive
component counts or merely validating the R/L word.

For AA and AC the explicit contradiction is at cell(0,-1): cut0 forces it
initially white; cut9's complete component would force it initially black
because it has already been read once. For CA and CC, the analogous witness
is cell(-1,0), initially white by cut1's complete component but forced
initially black by cut11 after its actual R birth. These latter conflicts
detect the surviving unread output singleton even when the turn word itself
