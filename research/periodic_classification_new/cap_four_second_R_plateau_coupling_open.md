# Open next coupling: first and second R waves on a cap-four maximum plateau

23 October 2026, 11:53 UTC. Research target only, NOT a theorem of exclusion.
All local count facts below are consequences of already signed stream tables.

The new first-discovery forest is unchanged by a tail reading only old wake
cells. It cannot by itself detect or budget nonstandard revisits. To close
cap four analytically, an explicit next layer is the rank-two/SECOND-R
chronology on a maximum clockwise-face plateau.

At cap four each active white cell has at most TWO R reads: ranks0 and2.
Let r_F,r_G be their counts around its two incident clockwise C faces. The
signed stream table gives h(F)-h(G)=r_F-r_G. Consequently:

- Equal-height active junction: (r_F,r_G)=(1,1), with3 or4 total reads.
  The FIRST R chooses one C face and the SECOND R chooses the OTHER.
- Height difference1: counts(1,0), with1 or2 total reads. There is no
  second R at that cell.
- Height difference2: counts(2,0), with3 or4 total reads. Both R reads
  turn around the SAME higher C face. These are the mandatory repeated-R
  defect cells at the relevant maximum.

At a GLOBAL C maximum M, the shifted-height inequality gives every adjacent
counterclockwise L-face height at most M-1. Thus its boundary edge flows
are positive and its corner vertices are active. A corner joining two
maximal C faces is therefore a genuine(1,1) junction, not an unused(0,0)
contact. On the max-C face-contact graph, first-R choices orient a flat
junction toward one face; second-R choices REVERSE this choice. At a
height-two boundary defect the choice does not reverse.

Both first-arrival and first-departure backbones stay in the max component,
but they only use rank0 and cannot control this reversed second-R wave.
The precise open task is to couple actual rank-two times and port ordinals
around the essential maximum component, with the forced same-face boundary
defects retained, rather than count first-forest roots or branches.

Do NOT assert a second-R functional map yet. A rank-two target's incoming
port occurrence can be supplied by a source L at rank1 or3, not only by
that source's second R. For example a two-read RL cell with opposite
incoming headings sends BOTH departures along one output port and can
supply a later target occurrence. Blindly matching R-to-R or replacing
these L suppliers by a second-R edge would be unsound. Those L suppliers
are the exact missing revisit coupling.

A last-exit tree is another exact interface: for any finite actual prefix,
last departures away from vertices other than the current head form the
ordinary rooted last-exit tree. At a white cell, a consecutive SAME-heading
return toggles its last-output port, whereas an OPPOSITE-heading return
leaves that port unchanged, because the color flips. These statements are
not a monotone budget: standard104 already has infinitely many ordinary
branches and last-output changes. A useful rule must isolate a NONSTANDARD
exchange and prove that it spends a resource not recreated by standard
wake growth. No such rule has been established here.
