## Why even the exceptional first V is invisible to the future context

The signed first-W theorem forces the first W base to be D. At a last-W head
(-A,1+B), VCC exits into E at (-A-4,1+B), and UCC at (-A-5,B). The following
plain V has local output y at most two. Any of its outputs meeting a future
context of minimum x=-3 therefore needs A<=1 for VCC or A<=0 for UCC.

Since the first W base is D, a boundary VCC/UCC cannot be the first base.
The UCC case is eliminated by A<=0. In the VCC case, A<=1 forces the entire
earlier W prefix to be exactly one plain D: A=B=1. Thus the first E V head
is exactly (-5,2,E). All six of its outputs miss each of the three exact
future contexts, giving 18 literal set-membership checks. Longer W prefixes
have A too large and are covered by the same cone, not a search cutoff.
