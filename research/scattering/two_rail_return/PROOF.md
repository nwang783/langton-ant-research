# Two clean U-turn rules do not compose through their discarded history

22 October 2026. This is an exact obstruction to a particular proposed finite
scattering reduction, not a counterexample to eventual highway behavior and
not an impossibility theorem for every finite-state model.

## Result

For every integer k >= 200 there is an explicitly specified finite initial
field B_k of exactly 3,877 black cells. The ant begins at (0,0), heading west.
The natural concatenation of two independently certified clean U-turn rules
would return it to this same pose after 208k + 42,448 updates. The actual ant
follows that planned word until update 208k + 42,296, then reads L instead of R
at (0,-2), heading west: exactly 152 updates before the proposed closure.

That obstructing cell is initially white. Its entire earlier read inventory
is time 4: R, time 8: L, time 100: R. Consequently the discrepancy is caused
by a black bit written during the original standard highway and not read for
208k + 42,196 updates. It is not an unmodeled extra input scatterer. Arbitrarily
large separation between the two designated crossing regions does not remove
this old-history interference.

## Completely specified input and planned word

Use the conventions and canonical 13-cell seed C, standard word W, and drift
d=(-2,-2) from `../transverse/PROOF.md`. Write R(x,y)=(y,-x).
Take the pristine crossing case (r,a,s)=(3,0,1), with n=20, whose twelve exact
settled black-line representatives (b,e) are generated by
`crossing.first_contact(3,0,1)[3]`. Each line is b+j e for integer j; the
representatives are obtained by parity from the literal W writes, not fitted
from a simulation picture.

Its certified clean reference trajectory reaches a standard NE highway at
phase 39. Remove those final 39 letters and prepend its undisturbed incoming
prefix. The resulting fixed word F has length 21,224, initial heading west,
terminal heading east, and displacement alpha=(-44,-2). Its SHA-256 is
b365447b3a00548e6af732e582f0a4f72fc77d8d512d097825880730098cafa9.
The checker binds the source recovery word to the previously certified
crossing record, and independently verifies all the new finite-field reads.

The input is the disjoint union

    B_k = C
       union {b + k d + j e : each of the twelve lines, -80 <= j <= 80}
       union {alpha + R^2(b + j e) : each line, -80 <= j <= 80}.

Thus its mass is 13 + 24*161 = 3,877. The two rail groups and C are uniformly
disjoint by their x+y coordinates. All input cells are installed initially;
no cell is supplied later during the motion.

The proposed concatenated word is (W^k F)^2. The first copy ends at
alpha+k d, heading east. The second is rotated by 180 degrees, so its
net displacement is -alpha-k d. Its formal endpoint is therefore the original
pose. This is a geometric closure only, not equality of complete fields.

## Exact all-parameter certificate

Run `python certify_first_failure.py`. The proof uses the previously audited
integer-affine XOR-field engine. It keeps the entire finite field and every
write from both k-period flights. The four checked pieces are:

1. W^k from the original pose;
2. all 21,224 letters of the first F;
3. W^k in the opposite orientation;
4. the first 21,072 letters of the second F.

Each fixed-word requirement is checked against the whole affine field.
Each periodic-run requirement is checked by cancellation of exact interval
boundaries, including all older writes. Every affine incidence and ordering
is valid for all k>=200, including isolated possible collisions at arbitrarily
large parameter values. The final cell is checked black while the next
planned letter is R. The chronology assertion separately exhausts all
periodic and fixed read sets, so the long age is not inferred from samples.

The successful checker output is `first_failure_certificate.json`. It records
5,362 fixed read constraints, 80 periodic read constraints, and more than
23 million excluded isolated equalities. These are proof-work counts, not a
finite parameter search.

## What reduction is ruled out, and what remains possible

The invalid composition discards an incoming highway's old initial history
and applies each clean U-turn rule as though its output corridor had the
pristine past required by the next rule. Here the geometric return is on the
same original standard track. The saved history affects a read before closure,
although both individual pristine scattering rules are exact and the gap is
unbounded.

A correct compositional theorem must propagate a read-avoidance guard against
all previously deposited material, or propagate enough information about that
material to predict its next reads. A designation such as “standard outgoing
highway, clean distant next rail” is not by itself a closed state description.
Our existing uniform single-crossing transfer lemma supplies such a guard for
one isolated crossing, but does not automatically supply it around this loop.

This does not show that the circuit is renewable. Nor does it rule out a richer
finite abstraction, a well-founded resource that tracks history, or eventual
capture of this family. The separate discovery script observed exact eventual
P104 capture at five finite k values; those observations are explicitly not
used as an all-k capture theorem. Proving the first failure is enough to refute
the proposed history-free rule composition.
