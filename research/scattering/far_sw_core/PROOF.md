# Negative-z arm: exact geometry and a reusable return interface

Status: the small exact checkers pass; independent review is pending. This
package does not close the negative-z arm.

## Only two asymptotic bands

For each fixed lattice site with z<=-94, the pristine exceptional rectangle
reads that site for all sufficiently large n exactly when its u coordinate
lies in [-28,-18] or [42,52]. The former is its initial blank SW leg; the
latter its NE return after q. The checker treats finite affine reads,
parameterized period intervals and their endpoint cases separately. A
nonzero determinant permits only an isolated n, not persistent incidence.
A line with zero z drift is excluded by the checked cutoff. The final NW
ray recedes past every fixed site as n grows. No uniform n threshold over
all fixed sites is asserted.

Consequently the old incidence coupling excludes fixed cores with z<=-94
outside those two bands: blank is inert, each exceptional parent eventually
misses the core, ordinary remote impacts are separated in the established
diverging z/u half-planes, and the final original rectangle captures. This
is the same fixed-site coupling used for the earlier pristine-band results.

The two bands each have22 fresh lattice-ray classes. The SW bases have
z in[-15,-8], and the NE-return bases have z in[-34,-27]. Thus z<=-434 is a
common conservative cutoff guaranteeing age m>=100 in either band.

## Exact reuse of the one-old-rail model

On the NE-return band put
  O=(6-2m,-43-2m), source time208n-104m+61927, heading east,
and s=O+Q_r, where Q_r is the phase-r canonical NE read. For m>=100 and
n-m>=100, the complete q_n field at this source is retained without deleting
any old points or finite rays. After translating by O and rotating
counterclockwise, the entire strip |u_model|<=200 agrees exactly with the
already certified NW-plus-one-old-SE-rail model used in far_se_core's final
band. In physical coordinates this is the unbounded strip |z-z(O)|<=200.

The rail offset (-21,53) rotates to(-53,-21), which differs from(3,-77) by
28(-2,2). Since that rail is bilateral, the models are identical, including
phase and parity. The exact interval-XOR checker verifies this on the whole
strip, not only locally, and rejects an extra point at depth10^60. The
entire preceding q-only read history has u<=56 and z<=35. The new core is
explicitly checked to be unread before its designated fresh phase.

Thirteen complete two-cell parents q_n+s_m inherit permanent standard
NW/SE capture, because their entire model futures stay inside the strip.
`return_transfers.json` gives their exact times, poses, phases and possible
later exceptional-ray parities. These are not complete five-cell transfers:
three other remote cells may still interrupt their outgoing NW/SE rays.

## Four complete rejoin families

For r=13,14,27,89, the full actual and pristine fields rejoin at the same
pose up to a finite scar, with delays20,120,132,32. The join precedes the
remaining m+7 NE periods. All local changed reads avoid a_n,c_n,d_n by
explicit affine u/z inequalities. The remaining NE reference reads avoid
the scar by its exact model geometry. After segment3, every read of q,
q+a and the full rectangle has z>=-93, while every scar lies strictly below
that bound for m>=100. Thus all four complete five-cell rectangle families
capture at728n+200304+delta with the original final pose. The global field
difference is kept, rather than erased after local recovery.

## The first-core obstruction

The initial SW band is different. Its fixed core is read by the blank ant
before the remote q. Hence one cannot merely add it as a passive marker to
the old q/a/c parent chain. Its singleton recovery has an outgoing standard
front, but the complete stationary archive includes an arbitrarily long
age-m trail and the earlier blank transient. A later phase72 remote impact
can return to that archive. Existing local scattering tables do not justify
discarding it or identifying it with one of the already checked endcaps.
This is a missing uniform endcap/return theorem, not evidence of a renewable
circuit and not a claimed impossibility result.
