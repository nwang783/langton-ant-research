## Useful result first

The entire historical last-exit graph is one arborescence, not a growing forest of independent roots. Its leaves equal its indegree-two vertices plus one. An old-site arrival q removes q's old last-exit edge q->r and adds the departing root's edge to q, giving the exact chronological identity

    change in leaves = [old indegree(r)=1] - [old indegree(q)=0].

New-site arrivals do not change the leaf count. This is an exact dynamical identity on the whole history, with all archives retained.

## The stronger gross leaf budget remains open

For an interval-reset last-exit tree, the candidate

    leaves <= old-cut BLACK inputs consumed + last-R outputs +1

is neither proved nor disproved. The output split into permanent P and future-consumed F, and the identities delta BLACK=P+F−C and delta C*=F−C, are bookkeeping only. They do not imply the leaf budget.

A valid partial injection maps a leaf to the predecessor of its last read, which has a later last departure. At a fresh endpoint the map is injective because every predecessor has only one outgoing edge left after its selected tree edge. The map extends to a partially defined injective chronological graph.

But following it does not supply resource charges: actual chains can contain several unmarked leaves and terminate at an unmarked WHITE→WHITE nonleaf. The empty-seed square interval5710..6042 gives one stopped chain. MT100's completed pristine drought gives275 unmarked starting leaves with stopped unmarked chains, among783 unmarked leaves. A concrete MT chain contains two unmarked leaves before an unmarked branch. Counts are not distinct terminal resources.
