# Repeated returns consume transverse room in a fixed crossing band

24 October 2026. AUTHOR exact consequence of the independently signed returning-lifetime permanent-output debt. Independent audit requested. This is a finite geometric packing inequality and a conditional fixed-module consequence, not universal capture.

## 1. Fixed-band inequality

Fix w horizontal or vertical lattice lines J, and an interval [T,U] on which the ENTIRE actual future-consumed current cohort C*(t) has size at most M, with w>M>=1. Choose N genuinely R-born, subsequently consumed lifetimes with births b>=T and deaths d<=U. For each chosen lifetime require a cut tau between a=b+1 and d such that EVERY line in J strictly separates p_tau from BOTH p_a and the lifetime's cell q=p_d. The same physical set J is used for every lifetime.

Let S be any finite set containing all ACTUALLY permanent R births on J during the union of their half-open intervals [a,d). One sufficient geometric way to obtain S is to contain the complete read footprint on J during these intervals. Then

    4 N (w-M) <= M |S|.                                  (1)

If the chosen intervals [a,d) are pairwise disjoint, the sharper bound is

    4 N (w-M) <= |S|.                                    (2)

No order or stack condition on arbitrary other lifetimes is needed. The word “permanent” refers to this actual infinite orbit, not a local template's predicted output. The finite set S need not be connected or a wall.

### Proof

For one lifetime, sum the signed per-line two-leg inequality over J:

    P_J + L_a(J) + 2 L_tau(J) + L_d(J) >= 4w.

Here L_t(J) counts current relevant lifetimes on these lines. Each L_t(J)<=M, so the lifetime creates at least 4(w-M) permanent R births on J. Sum over the N chosen lifetimes.

An actual permanent R birth at update s is charged only by marked lifetimes whose intervals [b+1,d) contain s. Every such marked lifetime belongs to C*(s); hence its multiplicity is at most M. If those intervals are disjoint, its multiplicity is at most one. Finally, two actual permanent births can never occupy the same physical cell: after the first permanent birth that cell is never read again, so no second birth there can occur. Thus there are at most |S| distinct charged permanent births. These observations prove (1)--(2).

For a vertical band of w columns and a transverse interval of W rows, |S|<=wW gives

    N <= M w W / [4(w-M)],

or N<=wW/[4(w-M)] for disjoint returns. At w>=2M these simplify to N<=MW/2 and N<=W/2, respectively. These bounds are independent of the distance of the peak beyond the band.

## 2. A genuine transverse-spread consequence

Suppose one orbit has L_t<=M eventually and infinitely many consumed R lifetimes with arbitrarily late births in a FIXED finite set Q, whose return radii are unbounded. Pass to an axis/sign subsequence with peaks escaping outward. Fix any w>M lines in that direction, all strictly beyond Q and its neighboring post-birth head positions.

Infinitely many of the selected returning lifetimes cross every one of these lines outbound and back. Applying (1) to arbitrarily large finite collections forces infinitely many distinct actual permanent black cells on this FIXED finite-width band. Their transverse coordinate must therefore be unbounded. More quantitatively, N selected returns require at least 4N(w-M)/M distinct permanent cells on the band.

One can alternatively select a temporally disjoint subsequence because the birth times tend to infinity and every chosen death is finite. Equation (2) then removes the overlap factor. In either form, the resulting permanent output stays in the actual field forever.

Unbounded consumed ages in such a bounded birth module imply unbounded return radii by the finite first-return clock already recorded in the signed debt note. Thus bounded true relevant stock plus arbitrarily old returns to a fixed module requires transverse permanent spread in every fixed sufficiently wide outward crossing band. This is not merely an increase in lifetime exchanges: it forces infinitely many distinct irreversibly occupied physical cells in a named spatial region.

The FIXED-module qualification is essential. If the birth modules migrate, their crossing bands may migrate too and this argument does not force repeated use of one finite transverse region. A standard highway's late birth cells drift away and do not satisfy the hypothesis. No arbitrary nontranslating finite-seed orbit has been shown to possess a recurrent bounded birth module, a transverse-width cap, or the relevant-stock cap.
