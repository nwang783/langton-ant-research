# First-contact geometry compresses to a bounded C window

## 1. Scope and the main distinction

Take an actual uninterrupted U-entry complete-eight prefix, its FIRST foreign A in {F,Q,B}, and an arbitrary finite suffix of ordinary RAW D/C/V/U atoms with their actual marker contexts. Use the true repaired proxy: one chord per ordinary atom, F's chord, and Q/B split at the real F56 fine cut. That cut is not assigned a marked core or a producer.

The signed first-foreign theorem makes the initial old+A proxy simple. Suppose a first later positive contact occurs. Choose its earliest time within the new ordinary chord, and assign an old vertex to its OUTGOING piece. The preceding proxy is then simple by the definition of first contact, not by an unproved induction about the suffix.

Let K count actual C intervals from an ordinary source ENTRY up to but EXCLUDING the contact atom ENTRY. Source C itself is counted; terminal contact C is not. For a source inside a foreign piece, K counts actual postforeign Cs before the contact atom entry.

Then the following necessary windows hold, independently of the number of D/V/U atoms:

| Source of first contact | Necessary K |
|---|---|
| Ordinary chord BEFORE A | 0 through 4, with endpoint refinements below |
| Earlier ordinary chord AFTER A | 2 through 6, with endpoint refinements below |
| Common F-prefix piece of A | 2 through 5 |
| Q tail piece | 3 through 6; 6 is disallowed for contact U |
| B tail piece | 3 through 6 |

The first row includes a fixed foreign half-turn. The second row does NOT: the closed subarc starts after the foreign. They must not be merged. In particular a postforeign D/C/V source can support a geometrically convex loop without forcing the earlier U-entry prefix inside it. No earlier-U exterior witness or old reflex-enclosure conclusion is imported.

This is a bounded relative contact window, not a bound on the complete postforeign suffix. A source created late after A may have arbitrarily many Cs before its own entry. The sixty necessary old-U second-contact states and prepared boundary controls remain consistent with this theorem.

## 2. Every principal join is retained

Put beta=atan(1/3), so 0<beta<pi/4. Define ordinary DEFLECTIONS from north by

    a(U)=0, a(V)=beta, a(D)=a(C)=pi/4.

These a values are not the direction-angle alpha notation in the older foreign-kink note. An ordinary source in heading h has actual chord direction pi/2-h*pi/2-a(source).

For any consecutive ordinary atoms x,y, the principal turn is exactly

    1[x=C]*pi/2 + a(x)-a(y).

All sixteen possibilities lie strictly between -pi and pi. They include negative joins and reflex changes; no common turn direction is assumed. Therefore all deflections telescope over arbitrarily many D/V/U atoms.

The true foreign vectors in an entry-N chart are

    F prefix: (-6,-2),
    Q tail:   (0,-4),
    B tail:   (2,-6).

Their directions are pi+beta, 3pi/2, and 3pi/2+beta respectively. The actual complete-eight/firstforeign pair theorem supplies previous-C bit zero. For a non-C preceding atom x and next ordinary y, every principal join through the genuine foreign pieces sums to

    pi + a(x)-a(y).

For example the Q internal turn is pi/2-beta, while its final join is -a(y). The B internal turn is pi/2, while its final join is -beta-a(y). Thus the fixed half-turn survives the kink, but its individual joins need not be positive.

The checker derives all vectors from actual RAW exits and verifies the sixteen ordinary joins, thirty-six conservative non-C foreign splices, and twenty foreign-piece endpoint corrections exactly. A formal angle A*pi/4+B*beta is encoded by the Gaussian-rational ray (1+i)^A(3+i)^B. Exact rational dot/determinant tests prove the claimed direction modulo 2pi; coefficient ranges for beta/(pi/4) in (0,1) select its unique principal lift. No floating angle tolerance is used.

## 3. The C-to-foreign wrap is an essential excluded case

For a preceding C, the first foreign join is -3pi/4+beta, not its unwrapped 5pi/4+beta. Omitting that -2pi changes the clock. All twelve C/foreign/ordinary combinations are checked explicitly.

Actual raw C,F/Q/B plus M11 are feasible on finite prepared boards. Hence there is no universal raw-C-to-foreign grammar prohibition. The zero previous-C bit used here comes specifically from the signed actual COMPLETE-eight-packet-to-firstforeign interface. It is not inferred from the raw C symbol, an internal marker, or a graph-only restriction.

This is why the theorem is stated for one first foreign preceded by the actual complete-eight prefix. It does not authorize a second-foreign or arbitrary seven-word extension with untracked boundary wraps.

## 4. Exact source-dependent open turns

Let T be the sum of internal principal turns along the source-to-contact subarc. Starting or ending inside a straight piece changes its length but not its tangent. Telescoping gives:

    preforeign ordinary source x:
        T = (K+2)*pi/2 + a(x)-a(target);

    postforeign ordinary source x:
        T = K*pi/2 + a(x)-a(target);

    source in common F-prefix:
        T = (K+1)*pi/2 - beta-a(target);

    source in Q tail:
        T = K*pi/2 - a(target);

    source in B tail:
        T = K*pi/2 - beta-a(target).

For a source in the F-prefix of Q/B, the remaining real kink is included in the displayed endpoint term. An old joint is assigned to the outgoing tail piece rather than counted twice. No collapsed whole-Q/B chord is substituted.

The preforeign formula counts Cs before AND after A. In particular, for an old genuine U source it bounds true preforeign age by K<=4, but a returned C is never retroactively added to that preforeign funding clock.

## 5. First-contact Jordan correction, including endpoints and overlap

The finite join checks exclude an immediately retraced adjacent segment: no actual ordinary/ordinary or foreign/ordinary join has angle pi. Together with the preceding simple proxy, this makes any new nonadjacent contact have a first positive onset. The old subarc from the selected outgoing source point to the current head, followed by the new partial chord up to that onset, is a simple closed polygonal curve. Every earlier internal intersection is excluded by the actual first-contact choice. Let delta be its closing principal turn, from the incoming contact tangent to the outgoing source tangent.

We have -pi<delta<pi, STRICTLY. Opposite tangents would put both incident curve portions on the same ray near the closing point. Their interiors would overlap BEFORE the alleged first contact. This is impossible even when the new contact is its terminal endpoint. In contrast, same-direction collinear first onset is retained: it has delta=0 and is not discarded as a parallel determinant failure. Outgoing old ownership also prevents assigning an old terminal point to its incoming piece.

Every source class above has T>-pi/2 for K>=0. Consequently T+delta cannot be -2pi. The Jordan turning theorem forces orientation +2pi, so

    pi < T < 3pi.

The strict inequalities are the endpoint/reflex correction. They do not require the closing vertex to be convex. Substitution into the five exact open-turn expressions gives the table in section 1.

For a preforeign ordinary source:

- K=0 requires a(source)>a(target);
- K=4 requires a(source)<a(target);
- K=1,2,3 have no further angular refinement;
- a genuine source C has K>=1 because its own C interval is executed before contact entry.

For a postforeign ordinary source:

- K=2 requires a(source)>a(target);
- K=6 requires a(source)<a(target);
- K=3,4,5 have no further angular refinement.

The checker reconstructs all forty-four source/target rows, with exact allowed K sets. There are no undecided beta-dependent boundary cases. These are necessary angular windows, not physical realizability certificates.

A geometrically convex four-C loop is compatible with the postforeign row: when its final contact atom is C, the fourth C is terminal and is NOT in K, so a four-C execution can have K=3. This illustrates why neither the fixed foreign half-turn nor the old U-source reflex-side argument can be transplanted onto a later D/C/V source.

## 6. What the compression buys, and what remains unbounded

Each candidate first-contact loop crosses at most seven ordinary constant-heading epochs, plus at most two genuine foreign pieces. For a preforeign ordinary source, K<=4 gives at most six ordinary epochs separated by A; postforeign sources need at most seven. The lengths of all those epochs remain unbounded. No active-token count, spatial diameter, port count, finite-state quotient, or decreasing field resource is inferred.

There is a useful moving-window consequence. After five postforeign Cs, a first contact cannot have an ordinary source before A. After seven postforeign Cs it cannot have a foreign-piece source either. Any later first contact, if it exists, must use a postforeign ordinary source within the last six-Cs window. This does not erase older field cells: they may still fund or obstruct actual reads.

For an old genuine U source, K<=4 implies true preforeign age<=4. The signed arbitrary-tail CONTACT-OR-LEFT-persistence result can therefore supply the SAME original LEFT pair at ENTRY of the global first-contact atom, without any atom-count cap. Its all-four-physically-alive conclusion from the special first return must NOT be iterated: only the separately signed arbitrary-tail LEFT statement is available there. Likewise the immediate E-return inverse-head guards do not automatically apply after a longer monotone postforeign run.

The remaining problem is a bounded number of unbounded monotone epochs with actual color/identity and core I/O. It is no longer an ever-growing succession of atom-number catalogues. This theorem does not prove arbitrary-tail simplicity, exclude postforeign convex contacts, or extend any old-eight maturity law through A.
