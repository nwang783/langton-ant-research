# First-L fuel versus recurrent white-writing events

22 October2026. Status: the qualitative two-visit theorem is independently
audited in ../core_periodicity_new/two_visit_tail_impossible.md. The quantitative
run ledger below was independently derived and cross-checked by the core and
computability analyses; the R-only form and the stronger coupled-charge version in Section4 are
now independently audited.
It is a global chronology restriction, NOT highway stabilization.

## 1. An all-prefix inequality

Consider any finite legal turn word of length t. Let m be its minimal initial
black fuel, the number of distinct cells whose first read is L. Let q_R be
the number of R reads which are third or later visits to their physical cell.
Then

    M = 6m+4q_R+8,
    E = 11m+8q_R+14,
    t <= 4E+(E+1)(2M+2)
      = 132m²+184m q_R+64q_R²+422m+296q_R+326.          (1)

The same inequality holds with m replaced by any larger actual initial black
mass. All counts refer to this finite word, not to unknown future behavior.

### The run ledger

Write r_j,l_j for numbers of maximal R/L runs of length j. Every run has
length at most4: five equal turns revisit their initial cell with the same
letter. An internal maximal length3 run is impossible, since LRRRL and
RLLLR each have equal first/last reads at the same cell and no intermediate
visit to it. Therefore r_3+l_3<=2, for the boundary runs only.

There are at most m+q_R NONFIRST R reads: a second R must be at an initially
black cell, and all further R reads are counted in q_R.

- r_4<=m+q_R+1. Unless the run begins the word, the fourth R reads the
  predecessor of its first read, so it is a nonfirst R. The charge is
  injective in the fourth-read time. The first run contributes the extra1.
- l_4<=m+q_R+1. If the first L of such a run is a first visit, charge its
  initial black cell. Otherwise the next read after the four-turn loop is
  a third-or-later R, charged injectively by that return time. A terminal
  four-L run contributes the extra1.
- l_1<=3m+2q_R+2. A first-L singleton uses one of m initial black cells.
  For any other singleton L at time v, let u be its IMMEDIATELY PRECEDING
  read at that cell, necessarily R. The incoming headings are equal or
  opposite by the pose-sector invariant. In the equal case their predecessor
  cells coincide; the read just before v is therefore nonfirst. In the
  opposite case their successor cells coincide, so the read just after v
  is nonfirst. Isolation makes that neighboring read R. Each nonfirst R
  is adjacent to at most two singleton Ls. The only failures are u=0 in
  the predecessor case and v=t-1 in the successor case, giving the extra2.
  This uses the previous R, not necessarily the first visit, and therefore
  allows arbitrary intervening reuse at every other cell.

Let n_R,n_L be total run counts, and c the signed excess R minus L. The exact
identity is

    c=2(n_R-n_L)-r_1+r_3+2r_4+l_1-l_3-2l_4.

Using n_R-n_L<=1 and the preceding bounds gives c<=5m+4q_R+8. Apply the same
argument to every prefix, with its fuel and q_R bounded by the full-word
values. Its black mass is at most M. Since c>=-m, rearranging the identity
also gives r_1<=6m+4q_R+8. Consequently at most E runs have length other than2.

Between exceptional runs, an ordinary stretch is RRLL or LLRR repeated,
plus at most two letters. Each full four-letter block follows a translating
self-avoiding axial path and consumes two distinct cells that must be black
at the stretch's entry. Therefore its length is at most2M+2. There are at
most E+1 ordinary stretches and at most4E exceptional letters. This proves(1).

## 2. Consequences for actual finite-support orbits

For fixed finite initial mass m, (1) gives

    q_R(t) >= max(0,(sqrt(t)-12m-20)/8),
    liminf_(t→infinity) q_R(t)/sqrt(t) >= 1/8.

For the first displayed bound put u=12m+8q_R+20. Then E<=u-6 and2M+2=u-2,
so the right side of(1) is at most4(u-6)+(u-5)(u-2)<=u². The asymptotic
constant1/8 follows directly from the leading coefficient64 in(1).

Thus finite support forces infinitely many genuinely recurrent WHITE-WRITING
reads, not merely infinitely many second visits. These are third-or-later R
read EVENTS, not necessarily distinct cells. Standard highways also have
many such events; no K-event decrease is obtained.

Setting q_R=0 gives the same quadratic two-visit bound

    t <= 132m²+422m+326,

and in fact proves it for the broader class having no third-or-later R read,
even if some initially black cells are read L,R,L. In particular every tail
contains a third-or-later R event relative to that tail's beginning.

## 3. Candidate filters and limits

Any proposed all-time ant geometry with q_R(t)=o(sqrt(t)) requires infinitely
many first-L cells. This applies before asking whether its word is automatic,
morphic, or generated by an expanding-lap controller. It excludes sparse
seam-revisit constructions even when they are not literally self-avoiding.

The full prefix inequality also lower-bounds the minimal first-L fuel from
computed geometric reuse counts. A convenient weaker explicit version is

    m >= max(0,(sqrt(t)-8q_R(t)-20)/12).

If a candidate has a fixed global cell multiplicity cap d>=3, then every
cell contributes at most floor((d-1)/2) such R events. Therefore the number
of distinct cells contributing q_R events grows at least on the order of
sqrt(t). Without a multiplicity bound or a separate transience theorem, no
conclusion about infinitely many DISTINCT repeatedly read cells follows.

For an automatic equal-cell relation, the predicate

    Q_R(t) := R(t) and exists u<v<t [EqCell(u,t) and EqCell(v,t)]

is automatic. A certified regular-language count q_R(t)=o(sqrt(t)) is thus
an exact negative finite-support certificate. The existing first-L finiteness
checker is already more general; this growth obstruction may supply a much
smaller structural explanation. No new finite-seed counterexample is found.

## 4. Audited coupled-charge improvement and a spatial consequence

The core analysis's further capacity coupling is now independently audited in
../core_periodicity_new/higher_reuse_count_bound.md, Section5. A noninitial
R4 run's charged final R cannot also receive a singleton-L charge on its
left, saving one charge per such run. The resulting stronger inequalities are

    current_mass+r_1+2l_4 <= 5m+3q_R+8,
    t <= 90m²+114m q_R+36q_R²+348m+222q_R+326,
    q_R(t) >= max(0,(sqrt(t)-10m-20)/6).

The earlier ledger above remains valid but is superseded quantitatively.
In particular its q_R=0 bound improves to90m²+348m+326. No further constant
optimization is needed for the structural application.

The independently audited midpoint inequality in rigid_reachability.md also
states

    m_t+m >= 2||p_t-p_0||_infinity-2.

Combining it with the new mass bound gives the spatial constraint

    q_R(t) >= (2||p_t-p_0||_infinity-6m-10)/3.

Thus a candidate's third-or-later R count must support BOTH its elapsed time
and its physical displacement. These are event counts, not distinct-cell
counts. Neither inequality forces stabilization or prevents renewable
information from being reused indefinitely.
