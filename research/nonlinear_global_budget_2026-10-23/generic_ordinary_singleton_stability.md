23 October 2026. New analytic corollary, submitted for independent review.
Not arbitrary perturbation stability, not a mass-two theorem, and not a
uniform defect budget for all masses.

## 1. Statement

Let X be ANY finite-support state whose actual future is eventually the
standard104 highway. Fix a certified standard phase boundary T, full drift d,
and the corresponding 22 fresh first-read frontier channels. There are
computable, X-dependent cutoffs on TWENTY-ONE channels such that adding one
initially black cell sufficiently far out on any of them gives another
standard-capturing finite seed.

The only excluded channel is the rotation/translation of the retained
phase72 channel, whose canonical head is base.pos+(-2,-8) for incoming
d=(-2,-2). Its local pristine scattering goes spatially back along -d and
can encounter the arbitrary old X archive. No conclusion is made for that
channel or for the finitely many shallower ordinary positions.

If a complete finite standard-tail certificate for X is supplied, the new
cutoffs are effective. No unknown capture search is part of their derivation.
The old initial field is preserved; no original black cell is erased.

## 4. Arbitrary old finite archives are harmless to non-opposite output

Now start from the arbitrary capturing finite seed X. A sufficiently far
initial black addition in channel phi is not touched before its prescribed
first contact. The full precontact state is exactly the X trajectory board
plus that unread black cell. Its centered complete board differs from
C_phi union {0} only in the receding old-archive set

    (-N d + A) union union_{k>=N-c} (S-k d),           (1)

where A,S are finite and depend on the complete state of X at T. This retains
all prelude debris and the finite leading end of every past highway write.
It is exactly the complete discrepancy formula proved in the companion
compactification note, without any extra escaping black cluster.

For each of the21 non-opposite channel responses, its complete future read
set is a finite transient A_phi followed by one standard ray family

    A_phi union union_{l>=0} (S_phi+l e_phi),

where e_phi!=-d. The cone argument proves this entire read set misses (1)
for all sufficiently large N. First-difference induction then makes the
actual perturbed finite seed follow this response FOREVER. Hence it captures.

Cutoffs are computable: obtain A,S from X's finite complete state at T and
its period. Obtain A_phi,S_phi,e_phi from the fixed stable channel anchor.
For every pair of their finite offsets, solve the integer ray-intersection
relations. Independent drifts give a unique pair of coefficients; equal
positive drifts give a finite upper bound on N because both ray coefficients
have the same sign. Finite transients give finitely many additional forbidden
N values. The only relation allowing unbounded cancellation is e_phi=-d,
precisely the excluded channel. Take one larger than all resulting bounds,
and also large enough that the original finite prelude misses the added cell.

## 5. Count consequence and limits of iteration

On each of these21 far channels the actual post-contact turn stream is the
same fixed response, independent of depth and of the old finite archive.
Thus this whole singleton-addition family has a finite uniform K count,
for example

    K(perturbed X) <= K(X) + C_phi + 10,

where C_phi is the actual K count of the fixed causal contact response, or
its conservative fixed-anchor length plus10. The extra10 allows the contact
history join. This is uniform over sufficiently large depths on ONE fixed
base X. Neither its threshold nor K(X) is claimed uniform over all bases.

The exceptional reverse channel is physically present and cannot be removed.
After several distinct obstacle clusters, several long old wake arms may
exist; a new ray can encounter an older arm even without reversing the most
recent incoming direction. The argument therefore must NOT be iterated as
if only the newest archive existed. A global nonlinear rank still has to
control those reverse returns and the whole growing archival geometry.
