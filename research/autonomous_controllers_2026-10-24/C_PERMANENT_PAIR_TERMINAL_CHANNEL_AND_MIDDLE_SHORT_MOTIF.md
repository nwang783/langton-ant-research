# Permanent-output C channels and the live middle-short motif

24 October 2026. Author proof and literal controls, independent audit pending.
This gives a local all-length ownership constraint without fixing the whole
C trace. It excludes some possible continuations of the four live middle-short
boundary types, but does NOT exclude any of the four types themselves.

## 1. Two permanent R outputs leave a one-way C channel

Suppose two consecutive actual R reads occur at adjacent corners p0,p1 of
one active C face F, and BOTH are the last actual reads of those physical cells.
They are genuine permanent outputs, not merely currently BLACK bits. List the
other corners p2,p3 in the clockwise cursor order p0,p1,p2,p3.

After the pair, the cursor is at p2. The initial exit from F is exactly one of:

    L at p2;                       R at p2, then L at p3.

An R at both remaining corners would next read p0, which is forbidden by its
permanence. There are no other choices before exiting F.

Every subsequent visit to this SAME physical F is exactly the contiguous
channel

    L at p2, entering F from the other incident C face;
    L at p3, immediately leaving F.

Entry across either permanent corner is forbidden. Entry across p3 would next
read permanent p0. Entry across p2 lands at p3, where R would also lead to p0.
Thus these two L reads are forced. No R can occur on any later visit to F.
This is valid for every excursion length and exterior multiplicity. It does
not identify separate translated faces or assume a period.

## 2. Exact owner demand, with its one real exception

For any q>=1 later visits, their2q L reads consume distinct BLACK lifetimes.
At least2q-1 of their preceding R writes occur AFTER the initial exit and on
the OTHER active C face at the corresponding physical corner.

If the initial exit is R(p2)L(p3), the first later entry at p2 consumes exactly
that R(p2). Every other consumed lifetime was produced after an intervening L,
and cannot be produced on F because F has no later R.

If the initial exit is L(p2), the first later entry already needs an external
R after that exit. The first later L at p3 may consume an older lifetime,
possibly written on F before the permanent pair. All subsequent consumptions
need external R writes after their preceding L. This is the one possible
exception, and it must not be discarded.

Here external means OUTSIDE THIS C FACE, not BLACK input, prelaid fuel, or an
initial-seed lifetime. The external R writes can be genuinely regenerated by
the same finite-seed ant. Nor may these local demands be summed over different
faces without tracking possible shared ownership-count multiplicities.

The bound is sharp on standard104. Its permanent pair R29,R30 at F=(-2,3)
initially exits by L31. Its two later channels are L115,L116 and L127,L128.
Their exact R owners are39,28,123,120, respectively. Exactly39,123,120 are
outside F and after the initial exit. R28 is the retained old internal owner.
Thus the standard WHITE controller genuinely realizes q=2 and exactly2q-1=3
. The lemma is not a no-input contradiction.

## 3. Application to all four middle-short boundary types

Use precisely the signed complete WHITE full-drift(3,1), growth8 branch with

    T0..7 = S,W,S,EEWE,SSNS,EEWE,NNNSS,EEEWW,
    T=3y-x, and FULL odd set {0,1,2,6,7,6c+1,6a+2,6a+4},
    odd3<=c<=a.

All higher histories and the period stay unrestricted. Physical ordinal
matching gives the actual consecutive permanent output pair T6.R4 -> T7.R4.
At positions(-6,0),(-7,0) it lies on C7=(-7,0). The remaining corners are
p2=T10 at(-7,1), p3=T9 at(-6,1). Section1 therefore gives:

Initial exit: T10.N/L, or T10.N/R -> T9.E/L.
Every later visit: T10.S/L -> T9.E/L, with no intervening update.

For all but the possible first exceptional lifetime, the actual owners have
headings T10.S/R on C11 and T9.W/R on C9, respectively. They are not assumed
first reads, initial roots, or different-basin producers. The histories at T9
and T10 remain unknown except for these exact suffix/owner obligations.

If there is at least one later visit, the last-exit forest successor of C7 is
necessarily C9. If there is no later visit, the initial exit gives successor
C11 or C9 according to the two stated modes. This locates a last-forest edge;
it does not replace its clock by a first-forest clock.

## 4. The corresponding first-entry alternatives

The complete lower histories also force the following exact WHITE alternatives.
Normalize C1=(-7,-2), C3=(-6,-1), C5=(-8,-1), C7=(-7,0), d=(3,1).

- C1 first activates through T4.L1 from C5, then reads T3.R0.
- C3 first activates through T3.L1 from C1, then reads T6.R0.
- C5 first activates either through T5.L1 from C3-d, OR through T8.L1
  from C9. The latter requires the complete T8 history to begin SS.
- C7 first activates either through T7.L1 from C5, OR through T10.L1
  from C11. The latter requires the complete T10 history to begin SS.

These are necessary alternatives, not converses. The SS prefix alone does not
say which candidate entry actually comes first.

For C5, the only other incoming named L is T7.L3, but C5 has already hosted
T7.R2 before it. A later L at T5 or T8 cannot be a WHITE first-C entry: the
signed first-entry lemma requires that selected cell's first L, rank1, with
its SAME-heading R0 owner. Thus an entry at T8 must use S/R0 then S/L1.

For C7, entry through T6.L1 is too late because T7.L1 has already entered
this same physical face. Entry across T9 would next read T6 from S, whose
first such read is rank3; that occurs after the already fixed C7 read T7.L3.
The only remaining corner is T10, again requiring its first L with SAME owner.
This argument uses complete T6/T7 ranks, not an exterior cap or a projected path.

If C5 takes its lower alternative, the three named first-parent edges really
form an embedded essential C-discovery cycle. In forward chronology its three
first-activation gaps are P-15,5,10. If C5 takes its upper alternative, no such
cycle is asserted. Neither case is excluded here.

## 5. The local word and early retirement are not themselves forbidden

The named successor graph contains an actual17-read channel

    RLRRRRLRRRRLLLLRL.

Starting at its third read gives the15-read word

    RRRRL RRRRLLLLRL.

Its first C face has the COMPLETE active history RRRRLL, at offsets0,1,2,3,4,12.
Its next C face has COMPLETE history RRRRLRLL, with its final L later. This
uses all four incident complete histories at each face; it is not a claim
based only on a cropped visit record.

The bare15-read word occurs at phase55 of actual standard104. That standard
face has15 complete active reads, first55 and last309, so treating the bare
word as early retirement would be wrong.

Even the exact early-retirement pair of faces occurs in the genuine blank
trajectory. At blank time7895 the word above starts, with COMPLETE first-face
reads7895,7896,7897,7898,7899,7907 and COMPLETE second-face reads
7900,7901,7902,7903,7904,7908,7909,7921. After a proper rotation/translation,
its eight physical cell histories are

    S,W,S,EEWE,SSNS,EEWE,NNNS,EEEW.

The first six histories agree exactly. The last two DO NOT: they are NNNS and
EEEW rather than the middle-short NNNSS and EEEWW. Thus this is a complete
no-initial-input local countercontrol to excluding the early-retirement motif,
NOT a realization of the four middle-short types or a repeated full(3,1) front.
The middle-short additional two permanent R reads are exactly what activates
the terminal-channel obligation in Section3.
