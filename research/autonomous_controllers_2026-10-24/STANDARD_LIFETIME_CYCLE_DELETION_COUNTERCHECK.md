# Balanced lifetime cycles are not physical deletion modules

24 October 2026. Exact finite surgery-family result for independent review.
This is a countercheck on a proposed simplification, not a classification.

The chronological lifetime boundary graph of standard104 has five internal
components: four have two event vertices and one has fourteen. Each component
contains whole consecutive R-to-L lifetime pairs and therefore has zero turn
excess. It is tempting to erase those components as irrelevant internal traffic.

Consider the precisely bounded surgery family: choose any nonempty subset
of these five event sets, delete those phases from the supplied turn word,
and leave every other turn in its original cyclic order. There are31 distinct
resulting words. This is NOT assumed to be a physical splice.

Every resulting word restores its heading, has nonzero full drift and still
has turn excess12. Nevertheless every one fails the exact stable-class color
alternation test. The certificate gives, for every case, a complete full-drift
class and two consecutive stable reads demanding the SAME turn. Repeating
that word is therefore impossible on ANY initial board, not merely on a
finite WHITE input. Neither a BLACK-first warning nor a capped simulation is
being used as an exclusion.

This covers genuine phase rotations and rigid rotations of this same surgery
by relabelling: deleting the same cyclic event sets commutes with changing
the cut. It does not cover other reconnections, reordered segments, or any
larger surgery family. It supplies no new highway uniqueness theorem.

The point is structural: balanced lifetime accounting is necessary but does
not preserve the whole ant trajectory when events are removed. The internal
components of the boundary graph must remain as actual chronological and
spatial obligations until a valid physical operation is separately proved.
