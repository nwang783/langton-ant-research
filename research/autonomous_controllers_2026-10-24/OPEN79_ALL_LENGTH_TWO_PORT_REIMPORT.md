## Proof of mandatory contact

B cannot be read inside J: its next complete incoming heading is E, requiring
the preceding read at C, which would already end the first-return excursion.
Suppose J also avoids all of K. At its end, C.rank9 turns L from S to E and
reaches B. The subsequent reads are then forced:

    C(S,L) -> B(E,L) -> U(N,R) -> Q(E,R) -> A(S,L)
           -> Z(E,L) -> X(N,...).

The U and Q turns are R because neither cell was revisited after its displayed
L. A's next owned turn is L. Z is in the complete named T9 history; any legal
E arrival there must turn L. If that E read has already been consumed, the
trajectory fails even earlier, not by choosing an unspecified alternative.
Finally X has not been revisited since its first R. Its next complete read
must arrive S, while the forced path arrives N. This contradiction proves
that J cannot avoid K. No field maximum, guessed white exterior or shorter
excursion bound was used.

## Proof of the two ports

Consider the first K read within J. It cannot be Q: either possible incoming
heading E or W requires the immediately preceding read at U or X, already
in K. It cannot be A: its next owned incoming heading S requires the preceding
read at Q, already in K.

If it is X, the complete next incoming heading is S. Its previous read was
its first R, so this next read is L. If it is U, incoming N would require a
read at B, already shown impossible inside J. Thus U must arrive S. Its last
read was the displayed L, so it now turns R. These are exactly the two listed
north-entry alternatives. This reasoning concerns ACTUAL physical first
contact, not a phase order or a projection that forgets intervening reads.
