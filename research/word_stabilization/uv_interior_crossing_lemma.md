# Interior crossing obstruction for consecutive mixed U/V runs

## Statement and scope

Consider any physically feasible macro factor X F Y, where X and Y are words
over U,V and F is Q or B. The heading of Y is opposite that of X. Normalize
X's heading to north. At macro boundaries use the coarse center

    z = p + (forward - right)/2.

The U and V center displacements are u=(0,4) and v=(1,3). A Q fold changes
center by u-3v and reverses the heading; a B fold changes it by -2v and
reverses the heading. Thus both center paths lie on the same affine lattice
generated by u,v. Their edges can meet only at lattice vertices or overlap;
there is no proper edge-interior crossing.

At a common center, suppose X has the same macro on its incoming and outgoing
edge, Y likewise has the same macro on its incoming and outgoing edge, and
the two macros differ (one is U, the other V). This is a transverse crossing
of the two lattice paths. Such a crossing is impossible if both center visits
have two whole macro blocks before the outgoing block and two whole blocks
after it within their respective runs.

Equivalently, the five-block windows centered at the two outgoing blocks have
forms abcde and fghij with b=c, g=h, and c != h. This is an all-length
necessary condition on consecutive mixed runs. It does not prohibit
nontransverse tangencies or shared stretches and is not a V-freshness proof.

## Exact local ingredients

Normalize the north-going central block's head to (0,0,N). Equal coarse centers
put the south-going central block's head at (-1,1,S). There are 128 possible
pairs of five-block windows: two choices of the straight crossing direction,
and three free U/V choices in each window.

`verify_uv_interior_crossing.py` walks the literal turn strings independently
of the stored footprints, verifies the endpoints and footprint binding, and
supplies for each of these 128 pairs a cell q with the following properties:

1. The last read of q in the north window and the first read in the south
   window have the same letter.
2. If s_N,e_N are the north window's start/end head y-coordinates,
   then s_N < q_y < e_N-1.
3. If s_S,e_S are the south window's start/end head y-coordinates,
   then e_S+1 < q_y < s_S.
4. q_y < e_N-5.

The explicit cells and read occurrences are preserved in
`uv_interior_crossing_certificate.json`. These are finite exact ingredients,
not a bounded simulation extrapolation.

## Shielding from arbitrarily long surrounding runs

A north-going U or V with head height y and displacement height h has read
y-range exactly [y-1,y+h], where h is 4 or 3. Consequently every block of X
before the north window reads only heights <= s_N; every block after the
window reads only heights >= e_N-1. Property 2 excludes all such reads of q.

After a half-turn, the read range is [y-h,y+1]. Every block of Y before the
south window reads only heights >= s_S, and every block after it reads only
heights <= e_S+1. Property 3 excludes all such reads of q.

The fold starts at the end of X, at height at least e_N. Both Q and B have
minimum local read height at least -5. Property 4 therefore excludes any read
of q in the intervening fold, regardless of how much X extends beyond its
five-block window.

Thus the two reads in property 1 are consecutive reads of the same physical
cell in the complete factor X F Y. Equal consecutive letters violate the
flip rule. This proves the stated obstruction on every coloring and for
arbitrarily long mixed runs.

## Stronger interior shared-center rule

The same proof gives more than transverse-crossing exclusion. At ANY shared
interior center of X and Y, the outgoing macro of X must be V, and both the
incoming and outgoing macros of Y must be V. The incoming macro of X is not
restricted by this necessary condition.

There are 1024 pairs of five-block windows in total. Exactly 896 violate the
stated rule. `verify_uv_shared_center.py` supplies a cell meeting the same four
shielding properties for every one of these 896 pairs, so the all-length
argument applies unchanged. The remaining 128 pairs are not claimed feasible;
the same elementary check actually excludes 72 more of them, but that more
complicated context dependence is not used in this clean rule.

This also has a directed propagation consequence. If a shared center is
interior in both runs, the earlier outgoing V edge reaches the same center
as the later incoming V edge came from. Moving forward in X and backward in
Y therefore produces another shared center, displaced by v=(1,3). Repeat
while both visits retain their two-block margins. Thus any interior shared
center extends into a common V corridor toward the intervening fold until
at least one visit lies within two blocks of its run boundary. The finite
boundary configurations have not yet been ruled out, and this corollary is
not a no-return theorem.
