# Symbolic and five-macro work, 22 October 2026

The highway conjecture is not solved. This analysis found no finite counterexample. The results below distinguish exact identities, explicit counterexamples, finite exhaustive evidence, and conjectures.

## Existing reduction recovered, not replaced

The core source `../sources/kehao95-core/factor_language/proof/ten.md` proves the finite-support ten-factor attraction theorem by exact feasible-window graphs and an all-parameter three-foldback obstruction. Raw ten-factor membership alone is not a symbolic periodicity criterion: the raw length-nine overlap graph has 79 vertices and 84 edges, with five branch vertices. Independent generation of semantic prefixes agrees with the source near its 150-letter graph. No new theorem is claimed for this reproduction.

The later recovered five-macro reduction is in `../continuation_2026-10-22/ant_continuation/factor_language/five_macro_status.md`. An infinite nonstandard no-adjacent-defect tail must eventually be a physically consistent concatenation of C,V,U,Q,B, subject to its actual port graph. Arbitrary physically consistent five-macro words overapproximate that graph. Excluding all finite-support infinite five-macro words would therefore be sufficient for this intermediate reduction, but would not solve the full highway conjecture: infinitely many adjacent forbidden ten-windows would still need exclusion.

## Exact center-coordinate simplification

Use the recovered before-marker convention, with physical head p, forward unit vector f, and right unit vector r. Put z=p+(f-r)/2. At a macro boundary:

- C keeps z fixed and rotates the heading left.
- V sends z to z+r+3f, with unchanged heading.
- U sends z to z+4f, with unchanged heading.
- Q sends z to z-3r-5f, and reverses heading.
- B sends z to z-2r-6f, and reverses heading.

These are exact algebraic consequences of the five verified endpoint poses. In particular, a word over C,V has center positions on the square lattice generated by (1,3) and (-3,1); C is a pure in-place quarter-turn on that lattice. The half-integral initial center is (-1/2,1/2).

The script `cv_lattice.py` computes all C/V occurrences of a specified physical cell by solving the exact determinant-10 lattice equations. This gives finite face/edge incidence data for a possible planar proof, not a global non-return theorem.

## Candidate positive-fuel lemma

Candidate: every non-C macro in every physically feasible finite concatenation has at least one L-read at a cell not read anywhere earlier in that concatenation.

If this were proved, any such concatenation from m black cells would contain at most m non-C macros. Since C^5 is infeasible, its total number of macros would be at most 5m+4. This would rule out every finite-support infinite five-macro tail.

The candidate is NOT proved. It is much stronger than the recovered finite-fuel hull conjecture. For a tail starting after an arbitrary original trajectory, "earlier" means since the start of that tail; first L-reads then demand distinct cells black in the complete board at that tail's start.

The exhaustive search `macro_zero_fuel` at fuel cap 50 and depth cap 40 completed after 177,322,178 feasible prefix nodes. Its deepest word had 38 macros, so the depth cap was not reached. No non-C zero-fuel block occurred. The raw log is `zero_fuel50_depth40.log`. The search exhausts words, not all arbitrary original-ant paths. It is finite evidence only; its C++ signed coordinate packing should be replaced by the unsigned-safe packing used in `search_freshness.cpp` before treating it as a reusable independently audited certificate.

Separate randomized depth-first searches reached 148 or more macros and hundreds of first-L cells. At least 16 million tested nodes found no zero-fuel non-C block. Such randomized searches are discovery only. Their shortest record logs and literal traces are saved locally.

## False stronger versions, with literal counterexamples

The stronger claim that a fixed nose cell is always fresh is false for Q. The word

UBVCVUVVVVUVCVUCVVVVUUQ

is physically feasible, has 567 turns, and needs exactly 89 initial black cells. Its last Q starts at physical pose (4,1), west, and its local (0,2) site is global (2,1). This site was previously read R at time 7, phase 7 of the first U. The final Q nevertheless requires 13 other new first-L cells.

A separate literal ant simulator verifies the full word on its exact first-L seed. The full seed and read trace are in `verified_UBVCVUVVVVUVCVUCVVVVUUQ.json`, generated by `verify_macro_word.py`. The word has no adjacent forbidden ten-windows and can be extended by C, V, or U while preserving that condition. Thus it is not merely a bad macro-join or terminal-overapproximation artifact.

A weaker topological shortcut is also false: a non-C macro need not have an L on a previously unvisited dual active vertex. The feasible word UBVCVUVVVVUVQVVCCV violates this shortcut in its final V while still consuming a new first-L cell. The sound implication is only that an L on a genuinely new active dual vertex must be a fresh first-L edge, because an old black edge's last R-read crossed both endpoints.

The V nose (1,2) and U nose (0,2), in their local before-marker frames, remained fresh in the tested deep samples. This is a separate unproved candidate, not supported by the false Q version.

## Active-motif potential attempt

A maximum of heights of locally executable markers (or of executable whole macros) is not monotone under macro execution. `active_motif.py` generates explicit finite counterfields. With zero orientation weights, there are 480 counterexamples among 948 minimal marker contexts and 2,878 among 5,576 whole-macro contexts. These are exact counterexamples to those particular zero-weight maxima, not an impossibility theorem for weighted or history-dependent motif potentials.
