# Fixed-drift finite palettes have an exact finite-front decision procedure

23 October 2026. Analytic architecture theorem, submitted for independent
review. This is an application of elementary finite-graph cycle reasoning,
not a new general automata theorem or a claim that the global ant is finite
state. No full graph for the1/5/9 palette is claimed implemented.

## 1. Supplied architecture and conclusion

Supply a FINITE set Sigma of nonempty literal ant words. Every word has the
same incoming heading h, restores h, and has the SAME nonzero displacement D.
The choice may change independently at every block occurrence. The union of
all relative phase-read positions is therefore a finite set E.

Supply a background input coloring T on the resulting infinite read strip,
periodic along D with known period p>=1. Equivalently, after choosing one
representative r_a for every full-D orbit meeting E, give

    T(r_a+kD) as a function of a and k modulo p.

Off-strip colors are arbitrary and never read. T may be an infinite prepared
rail profile; a finite EXTRA entry cap over T is not a finite initial board
unless T itself has finite support, for example T is white. Here a cap means
an arbitrary finite set of COLOR FLIPS relative to T: it may whiten an
initially black prepared cell. The simpler cycle test below is not asserted
for black-additions-only caps; that variant has its own reachability test.

There is an EFFECTIVE FINITE GRAPH deciding whether some infinite sequence
of Sigma blocks is legal on T with some finite extra cap. If one exists,
there is a periodic block schedule witness on T plus a finite cap. For a fixed
specified finite cap, existence is also decidable by finite-prefix reachability
followed by the same eventual graph. This is not a cap-launch construction from
a different finite seed or from standard producers.

## 2. Exact past/future overlap, not a heuristic memory bound

For each full-D orbit a, write every possible phase position as

    r_a+kD, k in K_a,

and put L_a=min K_a, U_a=max K_a. A possible phase read in block n is at global
orbit level n+k. At the beginning of block n, a relative cell level z could
have been read by an earlier block iff z<=U_a-1:

* an earlier block n-j, j>=1, reads relative level k-j<=U_a-1;
* conversely any z<=U_a-1 is a geometric possibility by selecting a potential
  phase at k=U_a and j=U_a-z.

This union is a relaxation of compatible earlier choices, which is harmless
for determining ALL cells requiring stored history.

A current or future block can read a relative level z iff z>=L_a, by the
analogous argument using a phase at L_a. Therefore the exact potential
past/future overlap is the FINITE front

    F_a={L_a,...,U_a-1}.

It has U_a-L_a cells. Levels below L_a can never be read again. Levels at least
U_a have not yet been read. The latter carry the supplied input T except for
an explicitly supplied finite cap.

Discarding a retired cell is only a state compression: the cell and its color
remain on the actual board and are proved permanently invisible. No debris
is erased, reset or replaced physically.

## 3. Exact colored-front transition

At a frame boundary store the colors on every F_a and the input phase n mod p.
For a selected sigma in Sigma, execute its literal reads, toggles and moves.
Every phase read has relative level between L_a and U_a. All but the top cell
U_a are stored front bits; the top cell is fresh and has color

    T(r_a+(n+U_a)D).

Multiple visits in the block use one evolving current-color bit. The edge
exists only if every literal turn agrees with its actual read color.

After execution advance the frame by D. New front level z corresponds to old
level z+1. Thus the updated front consists precisely of old levels

    L_a+1,...,U_a,

after their literal toggles. Old level L_a retires permanently. This gives an
exact transition for every supplied word and every stored bit assignment.

With m=sum_a(U_a-L_a), the graph has at most

    p*2^m vertices.

Edges may be generated by enumerating Sigma or symbolically from its finite
factored image choices. Variable word lengths do not affect the frame proof:
their poses agree, and within each block the literal order is retained.

The graph is exactly equivalent to real one-sided execution with its specified
front and fresh T input, by induction over blocks. It is not merely a local
factor overapproximation.

## 4. Cycle equivalence and computability boundary

Every graph cycle gives a periodic schedule. Its phase clock closes, so choose
a vertex on that cycle with phase0. Set the finitely many front cells to those
bits, leave every other cell at T, and execute. The exact transition induction
gives the infinite ant word. The cap changes at most m cells.

Conversely, any infinite execution over T plus a finite extra cap eventually
passes every cap cell it could ever read: for a cell in orbit a at global
level k, its possible read indices are the finite set k-K_a. After the last
such index, the execution has fresh T inputs and follows an infinite path in
the finite graph. Such a path reaches a directed cycle. Consequently unknown
finite entry caps do not produce an additional undecidability gap here.

For a GIVEN cap, process its finitely many exceptional fresh inputs through
their last possible read index, retaining a finite set of reachable front
states. Then decide cycle reachability in the periodic graph. Any cycle
reached after that prefix gives an ultimately periodic block-schedule witness
for the same supplied entry cap. Colors outside the potential strip remain
present but irrelevant.

Thus existence of an arbitrary nonstationary schedule in this architecture
reduces to finite graph cycle existence and has a periodic witness. This does
NOT say every accepted schedule is periodic.

### 4.1 Black-additions-only caps remain decidable, with a different test

For this restricted variant, initial front bits must equal T at background-
black cells, while background-white cells can initially be0 or1. Introduce a
CONTROLLED finite graph: at each step, each fresh background-white top cell
may independently be changed to black, and background-black tops stay black.
Execute each block exactly as before. A finite path in this controlled graph
uses only finitely many distinct fresh cells, so it is realized by finitely
many initial black additions. Conversely any finite black-addition cap gives
such a path until its last exceptional fresh input.

Let Z be the set of vertices that reach a cycle in the UNCONTROLLED periodic
input graph. A black-additions-only infinite realization exists iff Z is
reachable in the controlled graph from one of the allowed phase0 initial
fronts. This is a finite graph reachability test. After reaching Z no further
additions are used. A controlled cycle alone is NOT sufficient: it might
require infinitely many distinct added black cells. When T is white, all
phase0 fronts are initially permitted and the simpler cycle-existence test
already suffices.

The hypotheses are the boundary. Different block displacements or headings,
unbounded phase footprints/palettes, an input not periodic along D, or general
finite-seed ant traces do not inherit this finite graph. No global capture,
finite-seed universality, or computability classification follows.

## 5. A complete finite-horizon refutation exists when the graph is acyclic

If the graph is acyclic, every path has fewer than p*2^m edges. Hence a slab of
p*2^m blocks is impossible even when EVERY initial front bit is arbitrary.
The colored-front initial state grants exactly the potential inherited region;
it does not constrain its earlier physical realization.

This explains why finite UNSAT slabs can be conclusive rather than heuristic
horizon searches in a fixed directed architecture. A short slab proof can be
far smaller than the generic state-count bound. SAT of any shorter slab alone
still says nothing about infinite feasibility.

For a finite FAMILY of periodic input profiles, keep the selected profile
fixed as part of the state, or analyze each graph separately. It is unsound
to change its profile selector independently at successive blocks unless
that extra freedom is explicitly part of a relaxed model.

## 6. Relation to standard-flight preparation and the current short palette

Compatible standard-rail inputs meeting a supplied finite phase strip reduce
to finitely many anchored profiles/bundles. This is the same finite geometry
used by the independently signed selector encoding. For a coarse drift
parallel to standard rails, a common period makes their input phase finite.
If every corresponding finite-front graph is acyclic, some effectively
bounded arbitrary-front slab length T refutes all bundles.

Before using the physical lift, use the SAME common longitudinal physical
orbit gauge for consumers and parallel writers. If their primitive coarse
periods differ, group consumer blocks into the common standard period first,
including its finite input-phase clock. No comparison of unrelated orbit
representatives is used for charging cells.

The physical fuel/routing lift then uses disjoint new regions of successive
T-block slabs in this common gauge. On orbit a, slab b's new levels are

    U_a+Tb,...,U_a+Tb+T-1,

in the orientation of D. These sets are disjoint across b. Choose T at least
one more than the GLOBAL phase-offset range as well as the graph deadline;
then each marked endpoint/cap/intersection level affects at most two slabs.
The already signed black-persistence and expanded-envelope ownership proof
gives a linear initial-fuel-or-routing lower bound. There is no further
consumer-period search or automatic global-budget conclusion.

For the exact short inverse104 palette, the stored front has64 bits, matching
the independently reconstructed64 inherited cells. The generic bound is huge;
